## 01 · Market & Competitor Analysis

### Market overview
- **Demand shift: social-first, vertical, always on.** Brands have moved budget from occasional TV spots to a steady stream of Reels, TikTok and YouTube content. Volume went up and per-piece budgets came down, and that split the market in two. There is cheap, fast content, and there are a few hero films a year that have to carry the brand.
- **Supply shift: gear is cheap, taste is not.** Cinema cameras, gimbals and drones are now affordable for anyone, so the number of "videographers" has grown fast. Direction, lighting, story, sound and grading are what set people apart.
- **Local strength: a country that's on screen.** Tunisia has a long film culture. It runs the Journées Cinématographiques de Carthage, one of Africa's oldest film festivals, and its deserts, coast and medinas have drawn international productions for decades.
- **Regional pull.** Gulf, North African and European clients increasingly buy remote or travelling production. A trilingual (AR/FR/EN) Tunis-based filmmaker with competitive rates can sell well beyond the local market.
- **Disruptors.** Generative video and in-house content teams will take over generic, low-stakes footage. Work that depends on real people, real places and real emotion becomes worth more as a result.
- **Implication.** Compete on intention, not on price. Sit clearly above the crowded middle of the market while staying as quick and easy to deal with as a freelancer.

### Direct competitors
| Archetype | Strength | Weakness | How Ghaith wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Established production houses | Crews, equipment, track record | Expensive, slow, layered, director rarely with the client | Same standard, one creative lead from brief to grade, faster |
| Freelance videographers | Cheap, available, comfortable with social formats | Same look for every client, weak story and sound, no process | Clear process, story first, craft that shows in 3 seconds |
| Event & wedding studios | High volume, packaged pricing, emotional storytelling | Template edits, trend-chasing | Brand and documentary credibility, an authored look |
| Agencies with in-house video | Hold the client relationship and strategy | Video is a side service, often outsourced | Become the agencies' preferred director/DP partner |

### Indirect competitors
| Archetype | Strength | Weakness | How Ghaith wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-house phone content and UGC creators | Authentic, near-free, high volume | No production value | Make the hero films their volume content refers back to |
| AI video and stock footage | Instant, cheap | Generic, no real people or places | Real locations, faces and emotion |
| Photographers adding video | Client base and taste | Motion and sound are secondary | Moving image is the core craft |

### Competitive positioning
The axes are **Transactional footage ↔ Story-led film** and **Improvised ↔ Structured process**. Production houses are structured and fairly story-led, but expensive. Freelancers, wedding studios and UGC sit in the improvised half. **The top-right quadrant, story-led and structured with direct access to the director, is empty. That is where Ghaith Jenhani should be.**

### Strategic gaps
1. **Authorship at freelance speed.** Nobody combines a director's point of view with freelance agility.
2. **Process people can see.** Few local filmmakers show the path from brief to treatment to shoot to delivery, so buying film feels risky.
3. **A recognisable look.** Most local work follows trends and is hard to tell apart.
4. **Trilingual storytelling.** Work that sounds natural in AR/FR/EN is rare.

### Opportunities
- Brand films for **hospitality, tourism and F&B**, which sell atmosphere.
- An **agency partner programme** with a fixed-rate director/DP package and the agency's logo on the deliverables.
- **Service productions**: local DP and production support for foreign crews.
- **Hero film plus cut-down kit**: one film with 6–10 social cut-downs as standard.
- **Personal work as marketing**: short documentaries for festivals and YouTube.

## 02 · Audience

### Primary audience
- **The Marketing Lead.** Marketing and brand managers and founders at mid-size Tunisian and MENA companies in hospitality, F&B, fashion, tourism, real estate and tech. They commission 2–6 hero films a year. *"I need something that makes us look like the brand we're trying to become, and I need it on time."*
- **The Agency Creative Director.** Creative and account directors who sell the idea and need someone they trust to shoot it. *"I can't afford a shoot that makes me look bad in front of my client."*

### Secondary audience
- Cultural institutions, festivals and NGOs (documentary storytelling)
- Musicians and labels (music videos)
- International productions filming in Tunisia (local DP or second unit)

### Demographics
Age 26–50, university-educated, mid to senior roles. Urban: Greater Tunis, Sahel, Sfax, plus remote diaspora and Gulf clients. Work in French and Arabic, often English. Project budgets from mid four figures to five figures (TND).

### Psychographics
Aesthetically literate, ambitious for their brand, impatient with clichés. Proud of Tunisian culture but don't want folklore postcards. Risk-aware: one bad production costs them credibility inside the company.

### Motivations
Raise how the brand is seen quickly. Have a hero piece for leadership, investors and partners. Make content that performs and still looks premium. Work with someone who makes the job easier.

### Pain points
Suppliers who miss deadlines or disappear. Pretty shots with no story. Endless revisions and unclear pricing. Being shown the same look the supplier gives everyone else.

### Needs
A treatment before the shoot. A fixed scope, price and number of revisions. Every format from 16:9 to 9:16. A director who listens, then has a point of view.

### Desired identity
"The person who brought in the film everyone talks about." To be seen as a modern, international-level brand from here, and as a decision-maker with taste.

### Buying considerations (in order of weight)
1. Portfolio fit
2. Trust and reliability
3. Creative chemistry
4. Clarity of offer
5. Price vs value

## 03 · Brand Strategy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | To give the stories, people and places of this region the cinema they deserve. |
| **Vision** | A Tunisian filmmaker whose name brands across the Mediterranean and the Gulf trust with the films that matter most to them. |
| **Mission** | To make films with intention, from the first conversation to the final grade, that make people feel something and remember who made them feel it. |

### Values
1. **Intention.** Every frame, cut and sound is chosen for a reason.
2. **Craft.** Light, lenses, sound and grade done properly, including the parts nobody notices.
3. **Honesty.** True to the subject, with no fake emotion and no overpromising.
4. **Rooted.** Shaped by Tunisian light, places and people, and made to international standards.
5. **Reliability.** On time, on scope, easy to work with.

### Brand belief / worldview
> Attention can't be bought. It's earned one honest frame at a time.

### Positioning statement
For brands and agencies in Tunisia and beyond who need films that raise how they are seen, **Ghaith Jenhani** is the director-led filmmaker who brings cinematic intention to every frame, with the clarity and speed of working directly with the person behind the camera.

### Value proposition
**Cinema-grade films, one creative lead, no surprises.**
- *Functional.* Concept, shoot, edit, sound and colour in every format, on a fixed scope and timeline.
- *Emotional.* The confidence of showing a film you're proud of.
- *Self-expressive.* Being the brand, or the person, with taste.

### Differentiation (reasons to believe)
- Director and DP in one person
- The Treatment first: the client sees the film before it's shot
- A signature blue-hour and tungsten look
- One shoot, every screen (hero film plus cut-downs as standard)
- Trilingual: AR / FR / EN

### Brand personality & archetype
**The Creator (about 70%) with an Explorer's eye (about 30%).** An author who goes out and finds the story rather than staging one.
- *Is:* visionary, calm, precise, warm, curious, confident
- *Is never:* flashy, arrogant, trend-chasing, vague, salesy

### Brand promise
> **Every frame, intentional.**
> Nothing in a Ghaith Jenhani film is there by accident: no shot, cut, sound or colour.

### Messaging pillars
1. **Intention.** Talk about why a shot exists. *Proof:* treatments, shot lists, behind-the-scenes explanations.
2. **Craft.** Show the expertise the client doesn't see. *Proof:* before/after grades, lighting diagrams.
3. **Stories from here.** Tunisian places and people, without clichés. *Proof:* personal documentaries, festival work.
4. **A process you can trust.** Brief, Treatment, Shoot, Cut, Deliver. *Proof:* testimonials, timelines, the deliverables kit.

## 04 · Verbal Identity

### Tone of voice
Speak like a director on set: **calm, precise, warm, confident.**

| Scale | Position |
|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | slightly casual |
| Reserved ↔ Loud | reserved |
| Poetic ↔ Technical | slightly poetic |
| Serious ↔ Playful | serious, with warmth |
| Humble ↔ Confident | quietly confident |

### Communication principles
1. **Show the why.** Explain the intention behind a choice.
2. **Fewer, better words.** Edit copy like a film.
3. **Specific beats superlative.** "3 days, 14 locations, 1 film" persuades more than "an amazing epic production".
4. **People before gear.** Mention equipment only when it matters to the result.
5. **Same voice, three languages.** Write AR/FR/EN natively, never translate word for word.
6. **Quiet confidence.** One strong claim backed by a frame.

### Key messages
- **Core.** Films made with intention, from the first idea to the final frame.
- **To brands.** "Your brand already has a story worth filming. I'll find it, frame it and deliver it on every screen your audience uses."
- **To agencies.** "Bring me the idea. I'll protect it from treatment to grade, and make you look good in front of your client."
- **To cultural partners.** "Real people, real places, told with care and the craft they deserve."
- **To international crews.** "Your eyes and hands in Tunisia: locations, light and a crew that speaks your language."

### Tagline
- EN: **Every frame, intentional.**
- FR: **Chaque plan, une intention.**
- AR: **كل لقطة، مقصودة.**

### Brand vocabulary
- **Use:** frame, intention, light, story, craft, treatment, the cut, grade, place, people, blue hour, feeling, on time
- **Avoid:** epic, viral, cinematic vibes, best in Tunisia, 4K quality!!, cheap, content machine, trendy, guaranteed views

### Messaging direction (examples)
- **Bio.** Ghaith Jenhani is a filmmaker based in Tunis. He directs and shoots brand films, documentaries and music videos, and he wants every frame to have a reason to be there.
- **Instagram.** 5:47 a.m., Tozeur. Forty minutes of blue hour, one take that mattered.
- **Proposal opening.** Before we talk cameras, let's talk about what you want people to feel at 0:03, 0:30 and the last frame.
- **Not like this.** 🔥🔥 EPIC cinematic video for the BEST hotel in Tunisia!! DM for prices!!!
- **Like this.** A hotel is a feeling before it's a room. We filmed the hour when the terrace goes quiet.
