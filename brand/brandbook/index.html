01

Market & competition

The landscape the brand has to stand out in.

Starting point · 2025 → 202600:01:02:00

A strong mark with no system around it

The 2025 logo already works. The G is made of a C and a lowercase g, and an aperture with six blades sits inside the bowl. It is distinctive, it reads well small, and it tells you the profession straight away. What it lacked was everything around it. There was no strategy, no voice, no colour logic beyond one navy, and no rules. Every touchpoint had to be designed from zero, so the result looked inconsistent.

Kept

The symbol and wordmark geometry, redrawn from the original vectors. The Blue Hour navy (#002B5C). The stacked lockup.

Added

A horizontal lockup, an avatar, a second accent colour, a type system, a graphic language taken from cinema tools, guidelines and applications.

Working assumptions to confirm

This book assumes Ghaith works from Tunisia as a director and director of photography. It assumes his main work is brand films, commercials, documentaries and music videos, and that he also takes international service work. The market and audience sections are qualitative strategy based on those assumptions. Validate competitor names, prices and audience details with 5–8 client interviews before using them to make investment decisions.

01 · Market overview00:01:03:00

Video is now the default, and craft is becoming scarce

Every business in Tunisia and the wider region now needs moving images. Most of it is produced quickly and for one feed, and is forgotten within a day. That leaves room for a filmmaker who makes work people remember.

Demand shift

Social-first, vertical, always on

Brands have moved budget from occasional TV spots to a steady stream of Reels, TikTok and YouTube content. Volume went up and per-piece budgets came down, and that split the market in two. There is cheap, fast content, and there are a few hero films a year that have to carry the brand.

Supply shift

Gear is cheap, taste is not

Mirrorless cinema cameras, gimbals and drones are now affordable for anyone, so the number of "videographers" has grown fast. Equipment no longer sets anyone apart. Direction, lighting, story, sound and grading do.

Local strength

A country that's on screen

Tunisia has a long film culture. It runs the Journées Cinématographiques de Carthage, one of Africa's oldest film festivals, and its deserts, coast and medinas have drawn international productions for decades. Locations, crews and a tradition of cinema are real assets.

Regional pull

MENA & Europe as one market

Gulf, North African and European clients increasingly buy remote or travelling production. A Tunis-based filmmaker who works in French, Arabic and English and offers competitive rates can sell well beyond the local market.

Disruptors

AI and in-house teams

Generative video and in-house content teams will take over generic, low-stakes footage. Work that depends on real people, real places and real emotion becomes worth more as a result.

Implication

Compete on intention, not on price

The middle of the market is crowded and losing value. The brand should sit clearly above it, as director-led craft, while staying as quick and easy to deal with as a freelancer.

01 · Positioning, gaps & opportunities00:01:05:00

The empty quadrant: story-led and easy to work with

Strategic gaps
  • Authorship at freelance speed. Production houses have craft but come with layers. Freelancers are fast but have no point of view. Nobody owns both.
  • Process people can see. Few local filmmakers show clients a clear path from brief to treatment to shoot to delivery, so buying film feels risky.
  • A recognisable look. Most local work follows trends and is hard to tell apart. Few filmmakers have a signature colour and light.
  • Trilingual storytelling. Work that sounds natural in Arabic, French and English is rare, and regional brands need it.
Opportunities
  • Brand films for hospitality, tourism and F&B. Tunisian hotels, restaurants and destinations sell atmosphere, and atmosphere is what Ghaith films.
  • Agency partner programme. Offer agencies a fixed-rate director/DP package with their logo on the deliverables.
  • Service productions. Offer local DP and production support to foreign crews filming in Tunisia.
  • Hero film plus cut-down kit. One film delivered with 6–10 social cut-downs, so craft also covers the volume work.
  • Personal work as marketing. Short documentaries for festivals and YouTube build authority that ads can't.

02 · Profile & buying drivers00:02:02:00
Demographics
  • Age 26–50, university-educated, mid to senior roles
  • Urban: Greater Tunis, Sahel, Sfax. Diaspora and Gulf clients work remotely
  • Work in French and Arabic, often English with international clients
  • Project budgets from mid four figures to five figures (TND)
Psychographics
  • Aesthetically literate: they follow international campaigns and know good from good-enough
  • Ambitious for their brand and impatient with clichés
  • Proud of Tunisian places and culture but don't want folklore postcards
  • Risk-aware: one bad production costs them credibility inside the company
Motivations
  • Raise how the brand is seen, quickly
  • Have a hero piece to show leadership, investors and partners
  • Make content that performs and still looks premium
  • Work with someone who makes the job easier
Pain points
  • Past suppliers who missed deadlines or disappeared after the shoot
  • Beautiful shots with no story, or a story with amateur visuals
  • Endless revision rounds and unclear pricing
  • Being shown the same look the supplier gives everyone else
Needs
  • A clear treatment before the shoot: mood, frames, plan
  • A fixed scope, a fixed price and a known number of revisions
  • Final files in every format they need, from 16:9 to 9:16
  • A director who listens, then has a point of view
Desired identity
  • "The person who brought in the film everyone talks about."
  • To be seen as a modern, international-level brand from here
  • A tasteful decision-maker rather than someone who buys on price

The Explorer (secondary, about 30%) brings curiosity about places and people: the desert at 5 a.m., a stranger's face, the light in a medina alley. Every frame, intentional.

Four pillars. Every message stands on at least one.

Intention

Every choice has a reason. Talk about why a shot exists, not only how good it looks.

Proof: treatments, shot lists, behind-the-scenes explanations.

Pillar 2

Craft

Light, lens, sound, grade. Show the expertise the client doesn't see.

Proof: before/after grades, lighting diagrams, gear and set breakdowns.

Pillar 3

Stories from here

Tunisian places, light and people, filmed with pride and without clichés, for international audiences.

Proof: personal documentaries, location stories, festival work.

Pillar 4

A process you can trust

Brief, Treatment, Shoot, Cut, Deliver. Clear steps, clear scope, on time.

Proof: client testimonials, timelines, the deliverables kit.

Tone attributes: Calm (no hype, no exclamation marks), Precise (concrete nouns, real numbers), Warm (talks about people before equipment), Confident (states, doesn't hedge).

1 · Show the why

Explain the intention behind a choice. "We shot it at blue hour so the city feels like it's waiting."

2 · Fewer, better words

Edit copy the way you'd edit a film. If a sentence doesn't move the story, cut it.

3 · Specific beats superlative

"3 shooting days, 14 locations, 1 film" persuades more than "an amazing epic production".

4 · People before gear

Mention the camera only when it matters to the result. Clients buy feeling, not sensor sizes.

5 · Same voice, three languages

Arabic, French and English are each written natively, never translated word for word.

6 · Quiet confidence

Let the work speak. One strong claim backed by a frame beats five adjectives.

Visual identity

05 · Creative direction00:05:01:00
Concept

Blue hour, tungsten light

Every filmmaker balances two kinds of light. There is the cool daylight and blue hour outside, at around 5600K and above, and the warm tungsten practicals inside, at 3200K. The identity is built on that tension. Blue Hour navy is the calm and the depth. Tungsten is the spark of a human story inside it.

  • Look through the camera. Graphic devices come from the viewfinder: frame lines, timecode, the slate, letterbox bars.
  • Restraint. Lots of dark space, a single point of warm light. Like a well-lit frame, most of it is shadow.
  • Authorship. The aperture in the mark is the brand's eye. Everything is seen through it.

05 · Logo anatomy00:05:04:00

Two letters, one lens

① The C · initial of Ghaith

A heavy outer arc that opens to the right, like a frame that hasn't closed yet.

② The aperture · the eye

Six blade segments inside the bowl. This is the iris of a lens and the brand's point of view. Don't redraw or animate it independently except in the approved iris reveal.

③ The g · initial of Jenhani

The crossbar and descender turn the C into a lowercase g, so the two names become one gesture.

Wordmark

"Ghaith Jenhani" is set in Montserrat ExtraBold and outlined. The descriptor "Filmmaker" is set in Open Sans Regular and outlined. Both are vector masters: never retype them.

05 · Imagery direction & basic elements00:05:08:00

Shadow first, then one source of light

Light

Motivated, directional, often a single source. Blue hour and practicals. Avoid flat midday light unless it's a deliberate graphic choice.

Grade

Cool shadows toward Blue Hour, warm highlights toward Tungsten. Skin stays natural. Blacks are rich but never crushed.

Composition

Generous negative space and off-centre subjects, so headlines have room. Wide establishing frames alternate with tight details.

Subjects

Real people at work, hands, faces in profile and Tunisian places beyond the postcard. Never staged stock smiles.

Do

Grain, flares from real sources, silhouettes, rack focus, 2.39:1 crops for hero stills.

Don't

Heavy teal-orange presets, drone-for-everything, HDR halos, stock footage, emoji overlays.

06 · Layout principles, imagery & graphic elements00:06:04:00
Grid

12 columns. Outer margin is 1/12 of the format width. Gutters are 1/48. Content aligns to the left column edge, never centred by default.

Rule of thirds

Headlines sit on the lower-third line, as they would in a camera frame. Images carry the top two thirds.

Letterbox band

Hero images are cropped to 2.39:1 inside any format. The band can bleed off the edges and type never overlaps it.

Negative space

At least 40% of any layout stays empty. Dark grounds outnumber light grounds.

Imagery rules

Text on footage needs a Night scrim at 0–85%. Never place copy over faces. One hero image per layout.

Graphic elements

Frame corners at 1.5pt, in Silver Screen at 20–40% on dark. Timecode replaces page numbers. The aperture ring is decoration only.
