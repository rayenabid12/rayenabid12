# How children's clothing, toy and retail brands talk to kids and parents and tell their brand stories

Research date: 19 Oct 2026. Method note: Firecrawl was rate-limited (shared with parallel researchers), so these notes rely on WebSearch result summaries. Pages were not fetched in full, so quotes are excerpts as returned in search snippets. Several brands had thin or low-quality coverage; those gaps are flagged below.

## Clothing/retail: who do they speak to, what tone, what brand stories?

### Takeaway
Kids' clothing brands speak almost entirely to parents. They tell heritage or founder stories (Hanna Andersson, Mini Rodini, Petit Bateau, Jacadi) and pair them with quality, durability or "hand-me-down" proof and, more and more, eco messaging. The 2025-26 shift is away from idealised parenting and toward candid, "messy" realism (Carter's "Unwrap the Chaos"). French brands lean on child development (IDKIDS/Obaïbi), culture and heritage (Petit Bateau x Opéra de Paris), and children cast as teachers of adults.

### Cited Findings
**Carter's / OshKosh B'gosh (US)**
- Carter's holiday campaign "Unwrap the Chaos" (agency Laforce, published Oct 2025) shows kids playing with cookie dough and unravelling gift wrap — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/unwrap-the-chaos-carters-new-cmo-pushes-for-more-emotional-relatable-marketing/); [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/unwrap-the-chaos)
- New CMO Sarah Crockett argued that idealised motherhood imagery has been overplayed: "sometimes the mess is the thing that brings the brightest smile to our faces" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/unwrap-the-chaos-carters-new-cmo-pushes-for-more-emotional-relatable-marketing/)
- OshKosh B'gosh marked its 130th anniversary, with heritage storytelling built on its classic denim and overalls — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/unwrap-the-chaos-carters-new-cmo-pushes-for-more-emotional-relatable-marketing/). OshKosh has also done collab capsules for cultural relevance (OshKosh x Kith, 2021) — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210910005081/en/Iconic-Brands-OshKosh-Bgosh-and-Kith-Join-Forces-with-Exclusive-Capsule-Collection)
- Carter's new logo and marketing campaign roll out more widely in 2026, aimed at Gen Z parents (secondary source) — [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/financial-news/show/3341982-carters-rebrands-flagship-label-turnaround/). One analysis site calls the campaign "More Than Just Cute", which conflicts with the trade-press naming; low reliability — [DCF Analysis](https://dcf-analysis.com/products/cri-marketing-mix)

**Hanna Andersson (US, Swedish-inspired)**
- Founder story: Gun Denhart and her husband Tom left their jobs and moved to Portland, Oregon, for a "simpler life". They started in 1983 "with a telephone, a card file and inventory spread throughout the house", and from 1984 sold all-cotton clothing imported from Sweden and Denmark by catalogue — [Company Histories](https://company-histories.com/Hanna-Andersson-Corp-Company-History.html); [Hanna Andersson Our Story](https://www.hannaandersson.com/our-story.html)
- "Hanna-Me-Down quality" is a core brand claim, and the brand says it is "the #1 resold premium children's brand" — [Hanna Andersson](https://hannaandersson.com/our-story)
- The Hanna-Me-Downs resale marketplace launched in early 2023. It grew out of customer testimonials about clothes passed on to new wearers. Sellers get 70% of the sale price in cash or 125% as a gift card — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/hanna-andersson-resale-marketplace/643373); [Motherly](https://mother.ly/?p=193585)

**Mini Rodini (Sweden)**
- Founder story: Cassandra Rhodin created Mini Rodini in 2006 after struggling, while expecting her first child, to find well-designed kids' clothing made from sustainable materials — [Mini Rodini About](https://www.minirodini.com/ca/about-us)
- Eco tone is blunt and self-deprecating. The site calls the clothing industry "a dirty business. Heavy on pollution, slave labor..." and admits "nothing is sustainable but this is how we work with sustainability" — [Mini Rodini Sustainability](https://minirodini.com/sustainability)
- The founder's hand-drawn prints use humour to create an "eccentric style", and she owns brand storytelling and tonality. The brand has run upcycling since 2013 and reports more than 13 tonnes of material saved — [Mini Rodini About](https://www.minirodini.com/de/about-us); [Mini Rodini Sustainability](https://www.minirodini.com/es/sustainability)

**Petit Bateau (France)**
- The international brand platform "Liberté. Qualité. Durabilité." It links heritage, artistic expression and emotion. The source is a LinkedIn post by a jury member, so the detail is light — [LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/in/tkokeguchi)

**Okaïdi / Obaïbi / IDKIDS (France)**

It "works with experts in early childhood development to design its products and promote the well-being and emotional safety of all babies", and supports associations that strengthen parent-child bonds — [IDKIDS corporate](https://corporate.idkids.com/en/?p=3941)
- "Play and learn" workshops (creative classes, parent/child sessions) — [IDKIDS corporate](https://corporate.idkids.com/en/?p=3941)
- The "Okaïdi Planet Challenge" app is "ludique et pédagogique" and teaches children "à protéger la planète en s'amusant" (ocean challenges, waste sorting) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/oka%C3%AFdi-planet-challenge/id1224550409)
- The We Act For Kids action fund (since 2007) and the OKWORLD corporate foundation, which works with 25 NGOs on children's rights and the environment — [Okaïdi solidarity](https://okaidi.com/solidarity-actions); [IDKIDS RSE 2023](https://corporate.idkids.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/IDKIDS_-RAPPORT-RSE-2023.pdf)
- Jacadi is IDKIDS' most premium label. It is positioned upmarket in France and as luxury abroad, with a classic, "Belle Époque"-inspired style, for ages 0-12 — [Toute la Franchise](https://www.toute-la-franchise.com/franchise/jacadi); [FashionNetwork 2003](https://fr.fashionnetwork.com/news/Franchise-d-une-boutique-de-mode-enfantine,102722.html)
- Tape à l'œil designs for ages 0-16 and has more than 250 stores in France — [Businesscoot](https://www.businesscoot.com/en/company/tape-a-l-oeil)

**MENA retail: Babyshop (Landmark Group)**
- "World Without Walls" (Ramadan 2018, FP7 McCann Dubai) was an emotional film of a child walking through a world without walls. It was backed by an original song, a children's storybook, a Ramadan collection and 360° experiences. It was reported as the most watched and shared Ramadan ad by a retailer in 2018, with a 10:1 profit ROI, and won a WARC Prize for MENA Strategy — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/Babyshop_World_Without_Walls/126225); [LBB](https://lbb.lbbonline.com/news/world-without-walls-babyshop-sends-united-message-to-the-middle-east)
- "A World We Deserve" (Ramadan 2020, FP7 McCann) told the story "through the eyes and the words of children from around the world" sharing what they will remember from COVID-19, as a film and song — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/fp7-mccann-creates-a-world-we-deserve-for-babyshop/)

**Crocs Jibbitz**
- Jibbitz are framed as "unfiltered self-expression" and personalisation. A Middle East piece talks of "a language of Jibbitz charms" for "a fresh generation of kids and young adults". Crocs bought Jibbitz for about $10M (2006) — [Harper's Bazaar Arabia](https://www.harpersbazaararabia.com/culture/junior/crocs-latest-campaign-celebrates-youthful-uniqueness); [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/crocs-how-the-once-mocked-ugly-shoes-became-a-4-billion-brand-9763866)

### Inferences
- Clothing is bought by parents, so the voice is parent-facing. The child appears as the subject of the story (play, mess, imagination) rather than the addressee.
- "Durability / hand-me-down" is a strong proof point that works as quality, value and eco messaging at once (Hanna-Me-Downs, Petit Bateau "Durabilité", Mini Rodini longevity).
- French brands use cultural heritage and pedagogy; MENA work (Babyshop) uses Ramadan values, emotion, songs and a child's-eye point of view, and recorded strong ROI.
- Candid humour about parenting (Carter's) is a 2025-26 trend that suits Gen Z/millennial parents.

### Gaps
- Not covered by reliable sources this session: Gap Kids, H&M Kids, Zara Kids, Primark kids, Cyrillus, DPAM, Vertbaudet, Mothercare (incl. MENA) and Stella McCartney Kids campaigns and tone. Searches returned only shopping-guide or retailer listings.
- No source found for an Okaïdi programme or signature called "Planète Enfant". The verified child-development and eco framing is under Obaïbi/IDKIDS and the "Okaïdi Planet Challenge" app. Check okaidi.fr directly.
- No measured outcomes for Carter's "Unwrap the Chaos" or Petit Bateau campaigns.

## Toys: storytelling, purpose campaigns and outcomes

### Takeaway
Toy brands run purpose platforms (LEGO "Rebuild the World" and "Play Unstoppable", Barbie "Imagine the Possibilities" / Dream Gap, Hot Wheels "Challenge Accepted"). These are backed by commissioned research and aimed squarely at parents, while product and content storytelling (worlds, characters, unboxing) targets kids. The best-documented commercial outcome is Barbie 2015: #1 YouTube ad, 12.4M views, double-digit Q4 sales growth (self-reported).

### Cited Findings
**LEGO**
- "Rebuild the World" launched in Sept 2019 (agency BETC Paris). It was framed around creativity as a future-skills need, citing the WEF Future of Jobs Report (creativity among the top three skills from 2020) and LEGO-commissioned research in which 70% of UK business leaders agreed — [Brothers Brick](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2019/09/17/lego-launches-rebuild-the-world-campaign-to-inspire-creativity-in-kids-around-the-globe-news); [Markt-Kom](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=115084)
- Formats included short films with "cultural creators" (Mark Ronson, Simone Biles, Tomo Koizumi) and real-world brick makeovers of London landmarks such as the Piccadilly Circus fountain (giant brick shark, dolphin, crocodile). The 2020 film was "the third year" of the platform and centred on resilience — [LEGO newsroom](https://www.lego.com/en-my/aboutus/news/2019/october/rebuild-the-world-cultural-creators); [Culture Whisper](https://culturewhisper.com/r/kids/lego_london_architecture/14663); [Creative Moment](https://www.creativemoment.co/legos-head-of-creation-peter-johnson-on-the-creative-insight-behind-legos-rebuild-the-world-campaign)
- No hard KPI outcomes were found. The PHNX Awards 2020 entry is self-reported — [AdForum](https://sookmyunguniversity.adforum.com/award-organization/6701461/showcase/2020/ad/34630610)
- "Play Unstoppable" (March 2024) drew on a survey of 61,500 parents and children: 76% of girls are confident in their creativity, but this declines with age. Society is "seven times more likely" to describe girls' creative work as "cute", "sweet", "pretty" or "beautiful", and boys' as "brave" or "innovative". Formats: the short film "More Than Perfect", free workshops in LEGO stores and online for ages 6-12, and a parenting guide "10 Steps to Fostering Creative Confidence" with Jennifer B. Wallace. Later extensions included Philippines "creativity caravans" and a UK Youth Sport Trust partnership (2025) — [LEGO newsroom](https://www.lego.com/aboutus/news/2024/february/lego-play-unstoppable); [LEGO Creative Confidence](https://www.lego.com/en-gb/play-unstoppable/creative-confidence); [Malaya](https://malaya.com.ph/sports/the-lego-group-breaks-gender-stereotype-and-encourages-girls-to-play-unstoppable)
- Child-wellbeing principles: since 2021 LEGO has worked with UNICEF and the LEGO Foundation (the RITEC research project) on a "wellbeing by design" approach for digital products. Its Digital Design Principles help teams ask whether a feature is ethically sound "not just legal". LEGO also released a free design toolbox for the games industry in Nov 2024 — [LEGO Responsible Engagement](https://www.lego.com/en-be/sustainability/children/responsible-enagement-with-children); [IDEO](https://ideo.com/journal/a-look-inside-legos-digital-design-toolbox); [LEGO MSOM pledge 2025](https://www-bau.drcf.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/making-sense-of-media/media-literacy-update/the-lego-group---msom-pledge-updates-april-2025.pdf)
- The 2024 Play Well Study covered more than 60,000 children, parents and caregivers. About two-thirds of parents think digital play benefits development, but 60% worry about screen time. LEGO's Aug 2025 parent content "Tools, not rules" uses a playful, non-preachy tone on gaming conversations — [LEGO MSOM pledge](https://www-bau.drcf.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/making-sense-of-media/media-literacy-update/the-lego-group---msom-pledge-updates-april-2025.pdf); [LEGO newsroom](https://www.lego.com/de-at/aboutus/news/2025/august/tools-not-rules-how-to-rethink-conversations-around-gaming-in-a-playful-way-without-the-arguments-)

**Mattel: Barbie and Hot Wheels**
- "Imagine the Possibilities" (Oct 2015) showed girls role-playing as a professor, vet and coach in front of real adults (hidden camera). Per the Cannes Lions case study (self-reported): #1 video ad on YouTube in October 2015 with 12.4M views, a 30% rise in positive social sentiment, and double-digit Q4 2015 sales growth, "the first quarter Barbie saw growth in over three years" — [Cannes Lions / Love the Work](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/imagine-the-possibilities-41046)
- Dream Gap Project (Oct 2018) is a "multi-year global initiative" against the factors that stop girls reaching their potential. It funds research by NYU's Andrei Cimpian and highlights at least 10 female role models a year. No outcome evaluation was found — [The Drum](https://thedrum.com/news/2018/10/09/barbie-wants-close-the-dream-gap-between-young-girls-and-boys); [AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/campaigns/barbie-inspires-young-girls-to-bridge-the-gap)
- Hot Wheels "Challenge Accepted": the 2017 launch (BBDO SF) was a 60-second mother-and-son film framing daily challenges as resilience-builders. It ran during NBC's American Ninja Warrior so parents and kids would watch together — [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/watch-hot-wheels-teaches-kids-embrace-failure/1838669). The Oct 2024 platform reboot ("try, fail, repeat and grow", growth mindset) cited Mattel research: 77% of children aged 4-8 believe they can learn anything with effort, but 71% of parents step in on small challenges. It also launched a Challenger Spirit Fund with Lewis Hamilton's Mission 44 — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241016547476/en/Hot-Wheels-Ignites-New-%E2%80%9CChallenge-Accepted%E2%80%9D-Platform-Dedicated-to-Building-Resilience-and-Fostering-a-Growth-Mindset-in-Children); [Marketing Dive](https://marketingdive.com/news/hot-wheels-challenger-spirit-ad-campaign-mattel-brand/694583)

**Playmobil**
- After a 2024 restructure ("Moving into the future"), Playmobil launched its first global umbrella spots in Oct 2024. They show cross-theme stories such as "the princess calls the fire brigade to rescue a cat from the roof" — [Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/toy-market-news/playmobil-presents-new-umbrella-brand-campaign); [Mynewsdesk](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/de/playmobil-deutschland/pressreleases/grenzenlose-fantasie-playmobil-praesentiert-sich-mit-neuer-dachmarkenkampagne-2024-3350075)
- "The World Is Your Playground" (spring 2025, new phase Oct 2025): "everyday situations are transformed into extraordinary adventures through children's imagination", with standalone stories per theme world. It is explicitly dual-audience: films speak to children's creativity, while parents are reached through emotional relevance in everyday family life. It runs on TV, VOD, social and DOOH — [Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/toy-market-news/playmobil-brand-campaign-the-world-is-your-playground-with-new-commercials/)
- Playmobil: The Movie released in 2019 — [Interempresas](https://www.interempresas.net/Juguetes/339352-Playmobil-apuesta-por-el-contenido-animado.html)

**Sophie la girafe (Vulli, France)**
- Launched 25 May 1961, the feast of Saint Sophie, which gave the toy its name. Made from natural latex (craftsman Rambeau), first by Delacoste, later by Vulli in Rumilly. It was novel because animal toys were then mostly farm animals and pets. Growth came largely through word of mouth — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_the_Giraffe); [France Alumni](https://francealumni.fr/en/static/7496)

- Caution: Mediapart reported production moved to China around 2013 and French production ended by 2019. **Moulin Roty (France)**
- A community of friends in a former mill near Nantes (1970s), a cooperative (SCOP), with "La Douillette" plush (1978). Poetic themed collections ("Sous mon baobab", "Les Parisiennes", "Tout autour du monde"). The sources are low-quality retailer blogs with conflicting dates — [Alcuinfonds blog](https://alcuinfonds.be/blog/berceau-magique-sous-mon-baobab/)

**Tonies (Germany)**
- Founded in Düsseldorf by fathers Patric Faßbender and Marcus Stahl, who wanted better audio for their kids. Sources say 2013 or 2014. Hardware plus collectible figures model — [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/reviews/toniebox-review/); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/boxine)
- Parent reassurance messaging: screen-free, simple controls, safe volume limits, "hours of independent fun while parents enjoy peace of mind". Newer "Cuddle Tonies" plush figures — [Baby Bunting](https://www.babybunting.com.au/brand/tonies); [Pickr](https://www.pickr.com.au/news/2024/kids-stories-become-tech-savvy-screen-free-fun-with-toniebox/); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/audio-first-how-tonies-is-winning-the-battle-for-kids-attention/)

### Inferences
- The pattern for purpose platforms: commissioned research turned into a stat headline, a short emotional film, practical tools for parents (guides, workshops), a fund or partnership, and celebrity or culture creators. Parents are the persuasion target; kids are the heroes on screen.
- Tone toward kids in product content is imaginative and humorous (Playmobil cross-theme mash-ups, LEGO brick sharks). Tone toward parents is developmental and research-backed (resilience, creativity, wellbeing, screen-time reassurance).

### Gaps
- No reliable sources retrieved this session on Fisher-Price, Hasbro/Play-Doh, Nerf, VTech, Smoby, Djeco, or LEGO Ninjago/Friends lore. Slogans such as Barbie's "You Can Be Anything" were not verified with a source here.
- No independent (non-brand) measurement of LEGO "Rebuild the World", Play Unstoppable or Dream Gap outcomes. LEGO's annual reports were not retrieved.
- "Get the world playing": no source found tying this phrase to a specific LEGO campaign.

## Storytelling devices and dual-audience balance: what works

### Takeaway
Character lore (names, bios, birthdays) and collectible mechanics drive organic, kid-led spread: Squishmallows reports more than 100M units, mostly organic, and L.O.L. Surprise had 8 of the US top-10 toys in 2018 with no TV ads. Purpose and reassurance messaging wins over parents. The most explicit dual-audience executions put co-viewing into the media plan (Hot Wheels on American Ninja Warrior) or into the creative brief (Playmobil 2025).

### Cited Findings
- **Squishmallows character bios:** every character has a name and bio on its hangtag; names come first, then bios. Bios are written by Kellytoy marketing manager Elisa Vazquez, who wanted people to be able to "find a Squish with their name on it". Jazwares' Gerhard Runken: Squishmallows "wasn't just a plush" because "fans love these characters' bios". Fans collect "by squad, by scale, by type", and "every character we design, we design with purpose" — [Toy Book](https://toybook.com/squishmallows-specialty-toys-feature/); [Mojo Nation](https://mojo-nation.com/?p=36445)
- **Squishmallows outcomes:** Jazwares acquired Kellytoy in 2020. More than 100M units sold (an earlier figure was 86M). Chief brand officer Jeremy Padawer says growth is "almost all organic", with "tens of billions of impressions" from fan sharing. Also a YouTube animated series (Squishville, 2021) and CAA licensing — [Toy Book](https://toybook.com/?p=593506); [Licensing Source](https://licensingsource.net/caa-picks-up-jazwares-squishmallows-brand)
- **Ty Beanie Babies:** birthdays and four-line poems on tags from 1996. Small production runs, limited shipments and announced "retirements" created a secondary market. In 1999 a bear named "The End" came with a fan vote on ending the line — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Toys/BeanieBabies); [Rare Historical Photos](https://rarehistoricalphotos.com/beanie-babies-history/)
- **L.O.L. Surprise unboxing mechanic:** Isaac Larian was inspired by YouTube iPhone unboxing videos. Dolls sit in a ball under layers of packaging and were seeded to kid YouTubers, marketed "without a single television ad". The first 500,000 sold out within two months. By end-2018 L.O.L. had 8 of the top 10 US toys (NPD) and 5.1B YouTube views (Tubular Labs) — [Bloomberg via Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fretail%2Fhow-youtube-unboxing-videos-helped-kids-take-over-the-toy-industry-1.1623434651421); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/lol-surprise-hot-new-toy)
- **"Hero is the child":** Petit Bateau "Le cours d'eau" (kids teach adults), Babyshop "through the eyes and the words of children", Hot Wheels' kid overcoming challenges, and Playmobil's "children's imagination" turning the everyday extraordinary — sources above ([La Réclame](https://lareclame.fr/rebellion/realisations/le-cours-deau); [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/fp7-mccann-creates-a-world-we-deserve-for-babyshop/); [Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/toy-market-news/playmobil-brand-campaign-the-world-is-your-playground-with-new-commercials/))
- **Co-viewing media:** Hot Wheels 2017 was placed in American Ninja Warrior to reach parents and children together — [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/watch-hot-wheels-teaches-kids-embrace-failure/1838669)
- **Explicit dual-audience brief:** Playmobil 2025 films address children's creativity while reaching parents via "emotional relevance in everyday family life" — [Spielwarenmesse](https://www.spielwarenmesse.de/en/mag/toy-market-news/playmobil-brand-campaign-the-world-is-your-playground-with-new-commercials/)
- **Parent reassurance as a core message:** Tonies (screen-free, volume limits, "peace of mind"); LEGO "wellbeing by design" and "Tools, not rules" — [Baby Bunting](https://www.babybunting.com.au/brand/tonies); [LEGO](https://www.lego.com/en-be/sustainability/children/responsible-enagement-with-children)
- **Humour and realism toward parents:** Carter's "Unwrap the Chaos" — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/unwrap-the-chaos-carters-new-cmo-pushes-for-more-emotional-relatable-marketing/)
- **Eco with humility:** Mini Rodini, "nothing is sustainable"; gamified eco education in the Okaïdi Planet Challenge app — [Mini Rodini](https://minirodini.com/sustainability); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/oka%C3%AFdi-planet-challenge/id1224550409)

### Inferences
- Lore and collectible devices (names, bios, birthdays, squads, rarity, surprise layers) give kids a social currency to share. That produces user-generated content and organic reach, which is the strongest commercial evidence in this set (Squishmallows, L.O.L., Beanie Babies). The risk is speculative bubbles and scarcity backlash (Beanie Babies).
- Dual-audience balance works best as a split: the child is the hero and the play is fun on the surface, while a research-backed developmental benefit (creativity, resilience, wellbeing) or practical reassurance (safety, durability, screen-free) is the parent layer.
- Heritage stories must be true. The Sophie la girafe manufacturing controversy shows the reputational risk.

### Gaps
- No rigorous comparative effectiveness study (e.g., Effie/IPA meta-analysis) of kids versus parent-targeted tone was found this session.
- Kids' advertising regulation (e.g., CAP/ARPP rules on advertising to children) was not researched here. It is likely covered by another researcher.
- More MENA evidence beyond Babyshop (e.g., Toys"R"Us MENA, Centrepoint, Ramadan toy campaigns, Arabic-language tone) was not found.
