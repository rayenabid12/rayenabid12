# Regulation, Self-Regulation and Ethics of Marketing Candy/HFSS Food to Children: Branded Animated Kids' Series with a Mascot on Social Video Platforms

> Scope: rules a confectionery brand must consider when publishing a branded animated series featuring its own mascot and products, on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and similar platforms, in France/EU, UK, US (for reference), GCC (KSA, UAE) and the Maghreb. Current as of October 2026 where sources allowed. **This is not legal advice.** Several items below could not be verified against primary text (see each Gaps section). Items marked "[background, unverified]" come from the researcher's prior knowledge and were not confirmed by a source in this session.
>
> Method note: the search tool was rate-limited and several official sites (arpp.org, arcom.fr) failed to resolve on fetch. Primary texts were read in full for the EU Pledge commitment, the ARPP "Comportements alimentaires v4" recommendation and IAB UK's summary of the UK LHF rules. Other points rely on search summaries of official or law-firm pages.

## 1. EU-level and international frameworks (EU Pledge, ICC Code, WHO, AVMSD)

### Takeaway
Under the EU Pledge, a member candy company may not advertise non-qualifying products in media where 30% or more of the audience is under 13. This now covers company-owned social channels, YouTube and contracted influencers. **Company-owned "brand equity" characters, such as a brand mascot, are expressly outside the Pledge's character restriction.** Licensed characters, celebrities and movie tie-ins are covered. A branded series aimed at children featuring a candy product is therefore very likely "primarily directed to under-13s" and off-limits for EU Pledge members, whatever the mascot's status. WHO (2023) goes further: it calls for mandatory restrictions protecting all under-18s and explicitly targets persuasive techniques such as cartoons.

### Cited Findings
- **EU Pledge 2021 enhancement (applicable from 1 Jan 2022; strengthened nutrition criteria by 30 June 2022):** the age of a child was raised to 13 and the audience threshold lowered to 30%. Members must either advertise to under-13s only products meeting the EU Pledge Nutrition Criteria, or not advertise to under-13s at all — [EU Pledge, Our Commitment](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **Threshold history:** originally 50% of audience under 12, then 35% under 12 from 1 Jan 2012, then 30% under 13 from 2022 — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **Definition of "primarily directed to children under 13":** measured media where 30% or more of the audience is under 13. Where data are unavailable, companies consider "the overall impression of the advertising" and the target demographic of the media plan. On targeted media they should exclude under-13s, apply social-platform age filters, exclude "unknown audiences" (not logged in), and exclude content primarily appealing to under-13s, e.g. "certain child-appealing movies or cartoons, nursery rhymes, influencers, games or YouTube channels" — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **Covered media:** TV, radio, print, cinema, online, including social media, company-owned websites and video-sharing platforms such as YouTube, plus direct marketing, product placement, interactive games, outdoor, mobile, and contracted influencers or content creators. Packaging, in-store, point of sale and user-generated content are not covered — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **Characters:** "Marketing communications" include those "that use licensed characters, celebrities and movie tie-ins. Company-owned, brand equity characters are not covered by the policy." — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **2014 "creative execution" rule (from end-2016):** where no reliable audience data exist, advertisers consider the overall impression, so that a communication for a non-qualifying product "is not designed to appeal primarily to children" — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **No product marketing in primary schools** unless requested by or agreed with the school for educational purposes — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **The ICC Code and the ICC Framework for Responsible Food & Beverage Marketing apply to all members' marketing, whatever the audience age or product.** Required:
  - no encouraging or condoning excess consumption;
  - portion sizes appropriate to the setting;
  - no undermining of balanced diets and healthy lifestyles;
  - accurate depiction of taste, size, content and nutrition;
  - snacks not presented as meal substitutes.

  — [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/); [ICC Framework 2019](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/08/icc-framework-for-responsible-food-and-beverage-marketing-communications-2019.pdf)
- **Criticism:** BEUC argues the Pledge should also cover visible product mascots and brands, and says it currently does not — [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/food-marketing-children-game-over)
- **ICC Advertising & Marketing Communications Code (2024 revision):**
  - Article numbering changed. Children & teens now sit in a dedicated chapter, and the exact article number in the 2024 edition is unconfirmed (Article 20/21 and Chapter E are cited). Article 18 is now testimonials and influencer marketing.
  - Under the previous edition, Article 18 covered children (12 and under) and teens (13–17). Its principles: do not exploit children's natural credulity or inexperience, and do not undermine positive social behaviour.

  — [ICC 2024 Code PDF](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf); [ICC: What's new](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/whats-new-in-the-icc-code/); [Bird & Bird 2024](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/major-updates-to-iccs-advertising-and-marketing-communications-code); [ICC interpretation statement on advertising to children](https://iccwbo.org/publication/statement-code-interpretation-reference-guide-advertising-children/)
- **WHO guideline (3 July 2023), "Policies to protect children from the harmful impact of food marketing":**
  - Recommends comprehensive **mandatory** policies protecting children of **all ages** (CRC definition, i.e. under 18) from marketing of foods high in saturated fat, trans fat, free sugars and/or salt.
  - Recommends a government-led nutrient profile model.
  - Says policies should be broad enough to prevent marketing migrating to other channels, spaces or age groups, including digital.

  — [WHO news release](https://www.who.int/news/item/03-07-2023-who-recommends-stronger-policies-to-protect-children-from-the-harmful-impact-of-food-marketing); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/07/1138322)
- **WHO, persuasive techniques:** press reporting says the 2023 guideline addresses techniques appealing to children, such as cartoons, toys and celebrity endorsements — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/food/who-releases-policy-recommendations-to-protect-children-from-harmful-effects-of-food-marketing-90393)
- **WHO/Europe Nutrient Profile Model (March 2023):** classifies which products may be advertised to children — [WHO Europe](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/14-03-2023-new-who-guidelines-aim-to-protect-children-from-unhealthy-food-marketing)
- **AVMSD (Directive 2010/13/EU as amended by 2018/1808), Art. 28b(2):**
  - Video-sharing platforms must apply the Art. 9(1) commercial-communication rules to communications they market, sell or arrange.
  - Member States must encourage co- and self-regulatory codes of conduct (Art. 4a(1)) to reduce children's exposure to HFSS ads.
  - Under Art. 28b(1), platforms must protect minors from harmful content.

  — [legislation.gov.uk copy of Art. 28b](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2010/13/article/28b/data.html); [EAO/CoE presentation on HFSS](https://rm.coe.int/hfss-sophievalais/1680a6a76d); [Better Internet for Kids: AVMSD](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/rules-guidelines/audiovisual-media-services-directive)

### Inferences
- **The mascot loophole is narrow.** A brand-owned mascot escapes only the Pledge's *character* restriction. The series as a whole is still a "marketing communication" for the company's products. If it is placed on a children's YouTube channel, a made-for-kids playlist or an under-13 audience (≥30%), or by "overall impression" appeals primarily to under-13s, a Pledge member cannot feature non-qualifying candy in it.
- Sugar confectionery and chocolate will almost never meet the EU Pledge Nutrition Criteria. In practice a Pledge-member candy brand must design the series for a 13+/family audience, or keep products out of the content entirely.
- The safe route is a brand-entertainment series with no product depiction, no consumption cues and no calls to purchase, positioned for families and with audience controls.
- Even a non-member brand faces national self-regulators that apply the ICC Code (ARPP, ASA, etc.), so the ICC Framework rules are effectively baseline.

## 2. France: ARPP, health message, Gattolin, Studer, Arcom charter, Nutri-Score

### Takeaway
France layers several rules on top of the EU frame:
- **The ARPP "Comportements alimentaires" v4 recommendation (applicable from 15 June 2026)** applies to all ads depicting eating behaviour, explicitly including imaginary characters. It bans scenes of eating in front of a screen at home, incitement to excess or all-day snacking, implied exceptional performance from eating, and undermining adults' authority.
- **The ARPP Enfant recommendation** bans exploiting credulity and pester power.
- **A mandatory health message, or a 1.5% levy, applies to food advertising** (Code de la santé publique L.2133-1).
- **The Arcom 2025–2029 food charter** now brings in Google, Meta, TikTok, Snapchat and influencer bodies.

### Cited Findings
- **ARPP "Comportements alimentaires" v4: date and scope.**
  - Applicable from 15 June 2026; last modified 18 March 2026.
  - Applies to all ads depicting eating behaviour, "whatever the public", including ambassadors, influencers and "personnages populaires, célèbres ou ordinaires, qu'ils soient imaginaires ou pas", i.e. **animated mascots are covered**.

  — [ARPP Reco Comportements alimentaires v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP v4, eating occasions and messaging (rules 1/1–1/3):**
  - Whole meals shown must be balanced, and **breakfast and goûter (after-school snack) must always be shown as balanced** (1/1a).
  - Do not contradict, ridicule or minimise good eating or lifestyle behaviour (1/1c).
  - Nutri-Score, where mentioned, must be legible (1/1d).
  - No incitement to excessive consumption, i.e. eating "sans retenue" or in unreasonable quantities. Wording like "à consommer sans modération" is banned (1/2a–b).
  - Sport must not be a pretext for excess (1/2c).
  - Expressing pleasure and satisfaction is allowed (1/2d).
  - Between-meal eating may be shown, but must not encourage eating all day, and snacking must not be shown as a meal substitute (1/3).

  — [ARPP v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP v4 1/4: no consumption in front of a screen at home**, whether by people, influencers or imaginary characters — [ARPP v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP v4 1/6:**
  - no food-waste scenes;
  - no discrediting of sustainable food;
  - no stigmatising people for size or body shape;
  - sport scenes must respect sporting values.

  — [ARPP v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP v4 2/1, humour and imaginary worlds:**
  - Young children must not be led to believe a food gives exceptional performance in school, art or sport.
  - Humour, offbeat situations and imaginary worlds are legitimate if they stay clearly in the register of fantasy.
  - Characters, imaginary or not, inciting children to consume must never promote behaviour contrary to good eating and lifestyle practice.

  — [ARPP v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP v4 2/2:** ads must not devalue or minimise the authority or advice of the adults around children regarding the product, nor suggest those adults abdicate — [ARPP v4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **ARPP Recommandation Enfant (older PDF, based on the ICC Code):**
  - Do not exploit children's inexperience or credulity.
  - Do not create a sense of purchase urgency or present a purchase as indispensable.
  - Do not minimise price ("seulement", "juste").
  - Do not suggest possession gives a physical, social or psychological advantage over peers.
  - Do not ask children to judge products they cannot meaningfully assess.

  — [ARPP Reco Enfant PDF](https://www.arpp.org/IMG/pdf/Reco_Enfant.pdf); [ARPP Children Code (EN)](https://www.arpp.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Children_Code.pdf)
- **French law, as quoted by the Assemblée nationale:** ads must not directly incite minors to buy by exploiting inexperience or credulity, nor directly incite them to persuade parents or third parties to buy — [Assemblée nationale question](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE18540.pdf)
- **ARPP Digital recommendation:** where messages directly solicit children towards spending, the call must explicitly involve parents. Advertising must be clearly identifiable — [ARPP Reco digitale v4](https://www.arpp.org/IMG/pdf/Recommandation_communication_publicitaire_digitale_V4-2.pdf)
- **Health message, how it works (L.2133-1):**
  - Set by the loi du 9 août 2004 and the decree and order of 27 Feb 2007, applicable from 1 March 2007.
  - Food advertisers must either include a sanitary message or pay a 1.5% levy on net campaign spend.
  - Online, the message must appear simultaneously with the ad and be accessible while it is viewed, with reference to mangerbouger.fr.

  — [Eurojuris](https://www.eurojuris.fr/consommation/articles/mentions-sanitaires-dans-publicite-alimentaire-6291.htm)
- **Health message, current text:** the obligation covers drinks with added sugar, salt or sweeteners and manufactured foods — [AN question](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/12/questions/QANR5L12QE98931.pdf)
- **Health message, display:** the message must stay on screen for the whole ad and need not be read aloud (industry help-desk source) — [Cape help center](https://help-advancedtv.cape.io/hc/fr/articles/7778772521105-Que-sont-les-mentions-sanitaires-dans-les-publicit%C3%A9s-en-France)
- **Mandatory Nutri-Score in ads:** amendments during the 2026 social-security finance bill (PLFSS) debate would have required Nutri-Score on all food ads (one version by 1 June 2026). They are recorded as **rejected**, and no promulgated reform of L.2133-1 was found — [Amendement 458](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/1907/AN/458.pdf); [AS1659](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/1907/CION-SOC/AS1659)
- **Arcom food charter 2025–2029 (signatories and scope):**
  - Signed with the Health and Culture ministries' support.
  - New signatories include the platforms Google, Meta, TikTok and Snapchat, the influencer and creator body UMICC, and the fast-food body SNARR.
  - Internet, social media and influencers are now in scope.
  - Arcom will analyse children's overall exposure to food commercial communications across audiovisual and digital.

  — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/89516); [Que Choisir](https://www.quechoisir.org/actualite-publicite-pour-la-malbouffe-le-secteur-fait-encore-le-coup-de-l-autoregulation-n148026); [Arcom page](https://www.arcom.fr/en/find-out-more/legal-area/legal-resources/charter-promote-healthy-diets-and-behaviors-audiovisual-programs-digital-content-and-commercial-communications-2025-2029)
- **Arcom charter, NGO criticism (Feb 2026):** health NGO signatories (France Assos Santé, Ligue contre le cancer, Fédération des diabétiques) say the commitments are stalled. The independent exposure study was not launched, and harmonising platform and TV obligations is not progressing — [France Assos Santé PR, Feb 2026](https://france-assos-sante.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CP-Bilan-Charte-Arcom-23-fev.-2026-mis-en-forme.pdf)
- **Under the charter, ARPP, Meta and Webedia ran an influencer-awareness programme** promoting the ARPP "Certificat de l'Influence Responsable" — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/conseil/comportements-alimentaires-arpp-meta-webedia-s-allient-influence-plus-responsable)
- **Loi Studer (n° 2020-1266 of 19 Oct 2020; in force 20 April 2021):** regulates commercial use of the image of children under 16 on online platforms. It applies child-performer labour rules (prior authorisation) and requires earnings to be deposited with the Caisse des dépôts. Arcom has a "Charte Studer" — [Solidarités.gouv](https://solidarites.gouv.fr/proteger-les-enfants-influenceurs-decryptage-de-la-loi-studer); [santementale.fr](https://www.santementale.fr/2021/04/la-loi-encadrant-lexploitation-commerciale-de-limage-denfants-de-moins-de-16-ans-sur-les-plateformes-en-ligne-entre-en-vigueur/); [Arcom Studer Charter](https://www.arcom.fr/en/find-out-more/legal-area/legal-resources/studer-charter)

### Inferences
- **Script implications of ARPP v4 for the mascot:**
  - never eat candy while watching a screen at home;
  - never eat handfuls or binge;
  - no "the candy gives me superpowers to win the race or pass the test" unless clearly fantastical and not about real performance;
  - never show candy as breakfast or as an unbalanced goûter;
  - never show parents being outwitted or ignored to get candy;
  - no food waste (e.g. candy fights or throwing sweets away).
- Note the timing: v4 applies from 15 June 2026, so episodes published now fall under it.
- If an episode qualifies as food *advertising* (for a manufactured product with added sugar), French practice is to carry the health message and mangerbouger.fr reference for the duration, or pay the 1.5% levy. Whether a brand-owned web series counts as "publicité" depends on whether it promotes the product. In practice a branded series featuring the product is treated as commercial communication under ARPP and Arcom logic, and must be identifiable as advertising.
- If real child actors or child voice talent under 16 appear in brand content on platforms, Loi Studer and child-performer rules apply.

- Exact current wording of the four health messages [background, unverified]:
  - "Pour votre santé, mangez au moins cinq fruits et légumes par jour"
  - "…pratiquez une activité physique régulière"
  - "…évitez de manger trop gras, trop sucré, trop salé"
  - "…évitez de grignoter entre les repas"

  Variants exist for children's ads, and any updated texts post-2024 were not verified.
- Fine amounts for missing the health message not confirmed. The 37,500 EUR figure in the brief was not verified.
- **Loi influenceurs** n° 2023-451 (Delaporte/Vojetta) [background, unverified]: requires the "Publicité" / "Collaboration commerciale" label on influencer content and includes provisions on minors. Not verified in this session.
- "Loi Climat et résilience" (2021) relevance to food advertising aimed at children not confirmed. It mainly concerns environmental advertising.

## 3. UK: CAP/BCAP HFSS rules and the 2026 "less healthy food" (LHF) restrictions

### Takeaway
Since **5 January 2026** (voluntary compliance from 1 Oct 2025), UK law bans **paid** online ads for identifiable "less healthy" products (HFSS plus a category list; confectionery is in scope) at any time, and TV ads 5:30am–9pm. **Owned media, such as organic posts on the brand's own YouTube or TikTok channel, is out of scope.** Paying to boost, sponsor or seed that content with creators brings it back into scope. Brand-only ads that depict no specific LHF product are exempt. Separately, the long-standing CAP Code rules continue:
- no HFSS ads targeted at under-16s;
- **no licensed characters or celebrities popular with children in HFSS ads targeted at pre-school or primary children**, while **brand-owned "equity" characters are permitted**.

### Cited Findings
- **Scope:** paid online LHF ads banned from 5 Jan 2026, targeted at UK audiences, regardless of audience age. There is also a TV 9pm watershed (the ban runs 5:30am–9pm). Government asked for voluntary compliance from 1 Oct 2025 — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works); [GOV.UK consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/brand-advertising-exemption-from-advertising-restrictions-for-less-healthy-food-or-drink/advertising-restrictions-for-less-healthy-food-or-drink-on-tv-and-online-secondary-legislation-to-provide-brand-advertising-exemption-consultation)
- **Definition of "less healthy":** HFSS under the government Nutrient Profiling Model **and** within a category in the Advertising (Less Healthy Food Definitions and Exemptions) Regulations 2024. Confectionery is among the categories — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works); [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/uki/inspiration/fmcg/how-to-navigate-the-less-healthy-food-ad-ban)
- **"Paid-for" includes:**
  - paying creators, including through affiliate links;
  - boosting social posts;
  - gifting that results in posts;
  - paid sponsorship, advergames and advertorials.

  Paying a creative agency to *produce* content is not "paid-for" placement. **Owned media (brand website, organic social) is out of scope, but a brand "cannot pay to boost or promote that content"** — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works)
- **Brand exemption** (Advertising (Less Healthy Food and Drink) (Brand Advertising Exemption) Regulations 2025):
  - Brand and range ads are exempt if they do not depict a specific LHF product.
  - "Depict" covers name, packaging, product logo, jingle, audio cue, "clues" such as colours or ingredients, and realistic images of an unpackaged product indistinguishable from an LHF product.
  - Brand colours, fonts, logos and straplines are allowed.

  — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/brand-advertising-exemption-from-advertising-restrictions-for-less-healthy-food-or-drink/advertising-restrictions-for-less-healthy-food-or-drink-on-tv-and-online-secondary-legislation-to-provide-brand-advertising-exemption-consultation)
- **Brand names identical to a product:** if the brand name equals the full name of a specific LHF product, it cannot be used in paid ads. The exception is company or range names in use before 16 July 2025 — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works)
- **Other exemptions:** SMEs (under 250 employees), B2B, audio-only, non-UK-targeted ads, and digital out-of-home — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works)
- **Enforcement:** ASA, with referral to Ofcom, which can fine. In July 2026 the ASA reportedly referred or fined a food franchise for digital LHF ads — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works); [Food Compliance International listing, Jul 2026](https://foodcomplianceinternational.com/industry-insight/news/6298-asa-fines-food-franchise-for-running-digital-ads-for-less-healthy-products)
- **Existing CAP Code targeting rule:** a prohibition on targeting HFSS ads at under-16s remains in force alongside the LHF rules — [IAB UK](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/less-healthy-food-and-drink-ad-ban-what-it-how-it-works)
- **CAP Code rule 15.15:**
  - Licensed characters and celebrities popular with children must be used with a due sense of responsibility.
  - HFSS ads targeted through content at pre-school or primary children must not include licensed characters or celebrities popular with children.
  - The ban does not apply to **brand-owned "equity brand characters"** or to non-HFSS products.
  - Licensed characters may give factual generic nutrition and safety statements.

  — [ASA advice: children & food](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-food.html); [RPC on Chupa Chups ruling](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/asa-rules-that-chupa-chups-ads-dont-suck/)
- **ASA rulings:**
  - **Kerry Foods (Oct 2024):** a Cheestrings ad with Garfield breached the rules because the character was popular with under-12s around a new U-rated film — [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/kerry-foods-ltd-a24-1244924-kerry-foods-ltd.html)
  - **Swizzels Matlow (2012):** a confectionery website with child-friendly games using Scooby-Doo was in breach — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/Rulings/Adjudications/2012/8/Swizzels-Matlow-Ltd/SHP_ADJ_196069.aspx)
  - **Mondelez (2018):** not upheld, because the page was directed at adults — [ASA](https://asa.org.uk/rulings/mondelez-uk-ltd-a18-462118.html)
- **Criticism:** Nesta says the brand exemption leaves about 36% of food ad spend (£824m) untouched and that owned media are completely exempt — [Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/mind-the-gaps-food-advertising/); [WCRF](https://www.wcrf.org/about-us/news-and-blogs/the-uks-junk-food-ad-ban/)
- **Official guidance:** [CAP LHF guidance PDF](https://www.asa.org.uk/static/98d383af-0e0c-4fbe-9c920927a369c112/Less-healthy-foods-advertising-guidance.pdf); [CAP supplementary advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-and-resources/less-healthy-food-and-drink-secondary-advice.html)

### Inferences
- **What the brand can and cannot do in the UK:**
  - An organic, unpaid animated series on the brand's own channel can legally show LHF candy under the LHF law.
  - It remains a marketing communication under the CAP Code (non-broadcast), so it cannot be **targeted** at under-16s. If its content is directed at primary-age children, an HFSS product appearing in it is very likely a breach of the targeting rules even with an owned mascot.
  - Paid promotion of episodes, influencer seeding or creator collabs showing the candy would be banned outright.
- **The UK-safe model** is a mascot-led brand series with no specific LHF product shown or named (generic or non-LHF items only, or sugar-free variants clearly identified), aimed at a 16+/family audience. Promoting it with paid media relies on the brand exemption.
- The mascot is an equity brand character, so it avoids the licensed-character ban. Co-branding with a licensed property (film, cartoon, toy IP) in HFSS content aimed at children would not.

### Gaps
- Exact current CAP Code rule numbers (15.14–15.18) and BCAP 13.9–13.12 wording were not re-verified.
- Whether the ASA treats an owned-channel animated series as an "ad" for remit purposes depends on content. The ASA's online remit covers marketing communications on an advertiser's own website and social media (background; check the CAP Code's scope rules).

## 4. United States (reference): CARU, CFBAI, COPPA, FTC

### Takeaway
US rules are self-regulatory for content (CARU, CFBAI), with COPPA and FTC rules on privacy and deception:
- **CARU's revised Guidelines (effective 1 Jan 2022)** cover under-13s, including influencers, apps, games and "blurring". They require disclosures children can understand.
- **CFBAI participants** advertise to under-13s only foods meeting its Uniform Nutrition Criteria. Some, like Hershey, are participants.
- **The COPPA Rule amendments** took effect 23 June 2025, with compliance due by 22 April 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **CARU Revised Guidelines** (effective 1 Jan 2022):
  - Age scope raised to under 13, aligning with COPPA.
  - Cover digital media, influencers, apps, in-game ads and social media.
  - "Blurring", the mixing of ad and non-ad content, must be avoided, with ads made easily identifiable using contextual cues.
  - Material connections must be disclosed in a way children understand; best practice is audio plus visual disclosures, repeated in video.
  - Bans manipulative in-app tactics ("deceptive door openers", social pressure).

  — [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/childrens-advertising-review-unit-issues-revised-guidelines-for-advertising-to-children); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/carus-revised-guidelines-for-childrens-advertising-what-they-mean-for-marketers.html); [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/caru-releases-revised-childrens-advertising-guidelines/)
- **CFBAI:**
  - Uniform Nutrition Criteria (2018 revision, effective 1 Jan 2020) cap calories, saturated fat, sodium and added sugars.
  - The 6th edition moved scope to under-13s (implementation Jan 2023).
  - The 7th edition (May 2024) extended school commitments to middle schools.

  — [CFBAI](https://bbbprograms.org/programs/children/cfbai); [CFBAI principles](https://bbbprograms.org/programs/children/cfbai/principles)
- **COPPA Rule amendments:** published in the Federal Register on 22 April 2025, effective 23 June 2025, with compliance by 22 April 2026. FTC policy statement of 25 Feb 2026 on age verification — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)

### Inferences
- Even for EU/MENA-focused work, CARU's "blurring" and child-understandable disclosure standards are a good global benchmark for a branded series. On YouTube, the "made for kids" designation is the COPPA mechanism (see §6).

### Gaps
- No 2024 CARU guideline revision was found. The brief's "2022/2024 updates" are partly unconfirmed; the main revision was effective Jan 2022.
- CFBAI treatment of confectionery not confirmed; the researcher believes candy generally does not qualify [background].
- FTC 2019 YouTube/Google COPPA settlement details not retrieved.

## 5. GCC and Maghreb: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria

### Takeaway
The region has sparse, fragmented and often draft or voluntary rules, but it is moving:
- **Saudi Arabia:** SFDA has voluntary guidelines restricting unhealthy food marketing to children. In 2024 it notified the WTO of a draft *Regulation on Marketing Requirements of Food Directed to Children* covering **under-18s**. Final adoption is unconfirmed.
- **UAE:** the NMC/Media Council advertising content standards (2012) protect children generally. The GCC Food & Beverage Alliance pledge (≤12) applies. In February 2026 **Abu Dhabi announced a ban on fast-food advertising** within an HFSS marketing framework.
- **Maghreb:** HACA (Morocco) and HAICA (Tunisia) enforce general broadcast rules against exploiting children's credulity and harmful behaviour. No food-specific nutrient-profile rules were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Saudi draft regulation (2024):** SFDA published a draft regulation on Marketing Requirements of Food Directed to Children, covering advertising directed to children **under 18** for packaged foods and food-establishment meals (WTO TBT notification, Oct 2024) — [Food Compliance International](https://foodcomplianceinternational.com/industry-insight/news/4831-sfda-proposes-regulation-on-marketing-requirements-for-foods-intended-for-children); [WTO English text](https://docs.wto.org/imrd/directdoc.asp?DDFDocuments/t/g/tbtn24/are630.docx); [Arabic text](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2024/TBT/SAU/24_07192_00_x.pdf)
- **Saudi voluntary guidelines:** earlier SFDA "Guidelines for Marketing Requirements of Food Directed to Children" are described as **voluntary** in a USDA FAIRS 2023 report. A World Bank publication describes SFDA voluntary guidelines restricting unhealthy food marketing to **under-12s**. **This conflicts in age threshold with the 2024 draft (under 18)** — [USDA FAIRS via APEDA](https://agriexchange.apeda.gov.in/ImportRegulations/FAIRS%20Annual%20Country%20Report%20AnnualRiyadhSaudi%20ArabiaSA20230004.pdf); [World Bank](https://issuu.com/world.bank.publications/docs/9781464818288/s/16438656)
- **SFDA reform narrative:** post-2018 reforms include restricting marketing of unhealthy food products to children — [SFDA "Journey of Change" (2025)](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-10/JourneyChangeHealthTransrormationsV8.pdf)
- **SFDA ad pre-approval:** SFDA uses an electronic system to approve food ad requests and bans misleading ads (academic source) — [Karger, Saudi J Health](https://karger.com/sjh/article/3/1-4/147/856206/Food-Advertised-to-Children-A-Content-Analysis)
- **EMRO public-sector legislation (2020):** a measure identified 19 HFSS product types as unhealthy items that should not be advertised (EMRO review; country attribution in the snippet unclear) — [PMC review: Marketing to children in the Eastern Mediterranean](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158545/)
- **WHO EMRO position:** children of all ages should be protected from marketing of foods high in saturated fat, trans fat, free sugars and/or salt — [WHO EMRO expert meeting report](https://applications.emro.who.int/docs/WHOEMNUT316E-eng.pdf)
- **UAE, Abu Dhabi:** a ban on fast-food advertising was announced 27 February 2026 under Healthy Living Abu Dhabi. It is part of a framework restricting HFSS marketing especially where children could be influenced, alongside Nutri-Mark front-of-pack labels — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/fast-food-advertising-banned-across-abu-dhabi-under-major-health-drive/); [Lexis Middle East summary](https://www.lexis.ae/2026/03/05/auh-fast-food-advertising-banned/)
- **UAE, NMC standards:** Resolution No. 35 of 2012 on the Standards of Media Advertisement Content applies to ads on any media and bars content offensive to children. A 2019 Gowling WLG note says there are no other UAE-specific restrictions on advertising to children — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/fr/insights-resources/articles/2019/advertising-to-children-in-the-uae)
- **UAE, industry pledge:** the Advertising Business Group signed an MoU with the GCC Food and Beverage Alliance, whose members pledged not to target children aged 12 and below with products high in saturated fat, trans fat or sugar — [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/opinion/the-case-to-limit-junk-food-adverts-1.17331)
- **UAE, schools:** in 2019 the UAE banned nine "unhealthy" foods, including chocolate, from school canteens — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2019/01/30/No-more-chocolate-soft-drinks-or-fries-UAE-bans-nine-unhealthy-foods-in-school-canteens/)
- **Morocco (HACA):**
  - HACA found a snack-wafer TV ad to be prohibited advertising for content that exploits children's and adolescents' inexperience and credulity and could encourage imprudent behaviour.
  - Broadcaster cahiers des charges (SNRT, 2M) prohibit "publicité interdite", including incitement to behaviour harmful to health.

  — [HACA decision](https://haca.ma/fr/printpdf/3510); [Bladi.net](https://www.bladi.net/haca-campagne-meditel.html)
- **Tunisia (HAICA):** suspended misleading food-supplement ads (2020). No children-specific food rule was found — [Kapitalis](https://kapitalis.com/tunisie/?p=300310)

### Inferences
- **For KSA, plan for the strictest reading:** treat under-18 audiences as protected for HFSS products in case the 2024 draft is adopted. SFDA pre-approval of food ads may also be needed for paid or broadcast placements.
- **For the UAE:** follow the GCC F&B Alliance pledge (no HFSS marketing to ≤12) and NMC content standards, and monitor emirate-level measures (Abu Dhabi 2026).
- **Across MENA**, culturally sensitive content standards (religion, modesty, respect for parents) apply in addition. Ramadan and iftar advertising periods attract regulator attention (Morocco).

### Gaps
- Final status, effective date and exact provisions (characters, cartoons, digital channels) of the Saudi regulation are unknown; check sfda.gov.sa or the WTO TBT notification G/TBT/N/SAU.
- The GCC Standardization Organization (GSO) children's food marketing guideline was not found. The WHO EMRO nutrient profile model (2017) is believed to exist [background, unverified].
- UAE federal 2025 Media Law / Media Council content standards update not retrieved.
- An "Arab Pledge" or similar regional industry pledge beyond the GCC F&B Alliance was not found.
- No sources found for **Algeria**: ARAV, the audiovisual regulator, and Law 14-04 on audiovisual activity are believed relevant [background, unverified].
- Morocco Law 77-03 on audiovisual communication article numbers and any HACA children's-ad charter not verified.

## 6. Platform rules: YouTube / YouTube Kids, TikTok, Instagram/Meta

### Takeaway
- **YouTube:**
  - No food or beverage ads may run in or around "Made for Kids" content (since 10 Jan 2020, global, regardless of nutrition).
  - Made-for-kids videos lose personalised ads, comments and other features.
  - Paid promotions are allowed in made-for-kids videos on main YouTube only with a child-understandable disclosure, but **videos flagged with paid placement or endorsements are excluded from the YouTube Kids app**.
- **TikTok:**
  - HFSS ads must not target users 16 and under and must not carry a specific call to purchase.
  - Branded content for children's products is restricted to 18+ audiences in several markets.
- **France:** Meta, TikTok, Google and Snapchat are signatories to the Arcom 2025–2029 food charter.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube ad ban around kids' content:** from 10 Jan 2020, all food and beverage ads in and around "Made for Kids" content on YouTube Main are prohibited globally, regardless of nutritional profile — [WFA](https://wfanet.org/knowledge/item/2020/01/10/YouTube-prohibits-all-food-and-beverage-ads-next-to-kids-content-as-of-today); [Markey letter](https://www.markey.senate.gov/download/youtube-kids-ads?download=1)
- **The ban covers only paid ads around videos, not in-video branding or endorsements.** Researchers flag this gap: a 2022 Rudd Center study found 38% of kid-influencer videos featured branded food — [FoodNavigator 2025](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/08/14/how-are-food-endorsements-on-youtube-influencing-children/); [Rudd Center](https://uconnruddcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2909/2023/02/Rudd-YouTube-Study-PR.pdf)
- **YouTube help page on made-for-kids content:** paid promotions in videos directed to children must contain a disclosure understandable by children. Ads must not be deceptive, unfair or inappropriate for kids. No personalised ads or third-party trackers — [YouTube Help 10588440](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10588440)
- **YouTube Kids app:** videos are removed from the app when the creator flags a paid placement or endorsement — [Markey/YouTube letter](https://www.markey.senate.gov/download/youtube-kids-ads?download=1)
- **Made-for-kids features:** comments, Stories, live chat and the notification bell are disabled (2020 announcement) — [KGUN9](https://www.kgun9.com/entertainment/youtube-starts-new-ad-policy)
- **Contested enforcement:** a 2023 Fairplay/Adalytics complaint alleged personalised ads still reached made-for-kids channels. Google disputed this — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/08/23/business/youtube-improperly-used-targeted-ads-childrens-videos-watchdogs-say); [Adalytics](https://adalytics.io/blog/is-there-evidence-of-personalized-ads-on-made-for-kids-youtube-videos)
- **TikTok:** a spokesperson said HFSS ads "should not feature a specific call to purchase and should not be aimed at users aged 16 years and under" — [Geelong Indy](https://geelongindy.com.au/?p=76477)
- **TikTok market-specific branded content rules:** products designed for minors are restricted to 18+ audiences in markets such as the US, Canada, Australia and Mexico, and branded content must not speak to or exhort minors or look overly childish — [TikTok Branded Content Policy, market-specific](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/branded-content-policy-country-specific-requirements?lang=TH); [TikTok: Protecting minors](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/protecting-minors-on-tiktok-advertising-initiatives)
- **Platforms in the French charter:** Google, Meta, TikTok and Snapchat signed the Arcom 2025–2029 food charter — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/89516)

### Inferences
- **A candy brand's animated mascot series aimed at children will likely be "made for kids" under COPPA/YouTube criteria** (animated characters, child-appealing themes). Consequences:
  - no comments or personalised ads;
  - no food ads monetising it;
  - exclusion from YouTube Kids if paid placement is flagged.

  Since the brand itself is the advertiser, the series is effectively branded content and should carry clear disclosure.
- **Platform minimum ages:** TikTok and Instagram require users to be 13+. Content "aimed at" under-13s there is structurally inconsistent with platform terms. Combined with TikTok's under-16 HFSS rule, TikTok/Instagram versions of the series should be designed for and targeted at 16+/18+ audiences and avoid "childish" exhortations.

### Gaps
- Current YouTube Advertiser-friendly / Paid Product Placement policy wording on food in made-for-kids content (2025–2026) not verified.
- Meta/Instagram branded content policy on HFSS and minors not retrieved. Meta restricts ad targeting of under-18s to age and location only [background, unverified].
- The 16 vs 18 inconsistency in TikTok's policy text is unresolved; check the current TikTok Ad Policies "Food & Beverage – HFSS" page per market.

## 7. Ethical best practice and concrete do's and don'ts for writers of a branded kids' animated candy series

### Takeaway
Synthesising ICC, EU Pledge, ARPP v4, CAP, CARU and WHO:
- design the series as **family entertainment with transparent branding**, not product advertising to children;
- portray candy as an occasional, moderate, shared treat;
- respect parents;
- never use pester power or urgency;
- keep fantasy clearly fantastical;
- keep product depiction honest;
- disclose commercial nature in child-understandable ways;
- control the audience and do not paid-boost product-featuring episodes into child audiences.

### Cited Findings (sources for the rules underpinning the do's and don'ts)
- **Moderation and portions:** no encouraging or condoning excess consumption, and portion sizes appropriate to the setting — [EU Pledge/ICC Framework](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/); in France, no eating "sans retenue" — [ARPP v4 1/2](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **No meal substitution or all-day snacking; balanced breakfast and goûter** — [ARPP v4 1/1, 1/3](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/); [ICC Framework](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/08/icc-framework-for-responsible-food-and-beverage-marketing-communications-2019.pdf)
- **No eating in front of screens at home**, including by animated characters — [ARPP v4 1/4](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **Parents' authority:** do not undermine adults' authority or advice, or suggest their abdication — [ARPP v4 2/2](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **No pester power or direct exhortation** — [AN citing French law](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE18540.pdf); [ARPP Enfant](https://www.arpp.org/IMG/pdf/Reco_Enfant.pdf)
- **No urgency or social-superiority claims** — [ARPP Enfant](https://www.arpp.org/IMG/pdf/Reco_Enfant.pdf)
- **Fantasy must not imply real performance benefits** — [ARPP v4 2/1](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **Honest product depiction** (taste, size, content) — [EU Pledge/ICC](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)
- **Disclosure and no blurring of ads and content** — [CARU via Hunton](https://huntonak.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/childrens-advertising-review-unit-issues-revised-guidelines-for-advertising-to-children); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10588440)
- **No stigmatising body size; no food waste** — [ARPP v4 1/6](https://www.arpp.org/nous-consulter/regles/regles-de-deontologie/recommandation-comportements-alimentaires-v4/)
- **No licensed characters or celebrities in HFSS content for children (UK)**, while an owned mascot is allowed — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-food.html); [EU Pledge](https://eu-pledge.eu/our-commitment/)

### Inferences: writer's checklist (derived from the above; not a legal opinion)

**DO**
- Build stories around the mascot's personality, friendship, curiosity, problem-solving and humour. The brand world, not the product, is the hero.
- If candy appears at all:
  - show one sensible portion, shared, on a clear occasion (celebration, after a balanced meal), with pleasure expressed but not excess;
  - prefer sugar-free or smaller-format variants and identify them clearly;
  - in the UK, identify a non-LHF variant by name or pack.
- Show parents and adults as trusted, respected figures whose rules the mascot follows ("ask a grown-up").
- Weave in positive cues naturally: active play, sleep, water, tooth-brushing after sweets, fruit and veg at mealtimes, without moralising or ridiculing.
- Keep magic and superpowers obviously fantastical and **not caused by eating the candy**.
- Label commercial nature clearly and age-appropriately, in audio and visual form. Examples: an opening card or voice line "A [Brand] cartoon", a channel description and in-video disclosure, and the platform's paid-promotion or branded-content toggles.
- Set the correct audience designation: YouTube "made for kids" if child-directed, which accepts lost features. If positioning for families or teens, write and target accordingly and apply age filters (EU Pledge: exclude under-13s and unknown audiences).
- In France, add the mangerbouger.fr health message on episodes that function as food ads.
- Run every script against ARPP v4 (from 15 June 2026), ARPP Enfant, and the ARPP Digital/Influence rules. Consider ARPP pre-clearance (*avis préalable*).
- Keep an audience-composition record (analytics showing under-13 share below 30%) per channel.

**DON'T**
- Don't show binge-eating, piles of candy, "can't stop eating", candy as breakfast or a meal, or eating all day.
- Don't show the mascot or kids eating candy while watching TV, a tablet or a game at home (France).
- Don't have characters nag, trick or outwit parents to get candy. Don't use "Ask your mum to buy…" or "Go get yours now!", countdowns, "only €1", or "everyone has one".
- Don't imply candy makes you stronger, smarter, more popular, braver or better at sport or school.
- Don't use licensed characters, celebrities, kid influencers or movie tie-ins alongside HFSS products in child-directed content (UK ban; EU Pledge covers them). If real children under 16 appear, comply with Loi Studer and child-performer rules.
- Don't mix ads into story without cues (blurring). Avoid "secret" in-story product placement, advergame-like mechanics, collect-them-all pressure, or rewards for purchase in child-directed episodes.
- Don't paid-boost, sponsor or creator-seed product-featuring episodes in the UK. That makes them paid LHF ads and banned. Under the EU Pledge, don't place them in media with 30% or more under-13s.
- Don't show food waste, fat-shaming or body-image jokes.
- Don't run product-featuring candy content targeted at under-16s on TikTok, or at under-18s in KSA if the draft regulation is adopted.

### Gaps
- No single source gives an industry-standard "writer's bible" for confectionery branded entertainment. The checklist above is a synthesis.
- Sugar-free gum and dental-health messaging rules (e.g. EU-authorised health claims for sugar-free gum, Reg. 432/2012) were not researched in this session.
- Legal characterisation of an owned-channel web series as "advertising" varies by jurisdiction and content. Obtain local counsel or regulator pre-clearance (ARPP, CAP Copy Advice, SFDA) before launch.
