# Kids' food, candy, snack and chewing-gum brands: tone of voice, mascots, origin stories and storytelling devices

Research date: 19 Oct 2026. Rate limits on the search tools held this to about 17 calls, so the work went deep on France and bubble gum (Malabar, Carambar, Big Babol, Bazooka, Boomer, Bubbaloo) and on the best-documented global cases (Haribo, M&M's, Kinder, Oreo, Chupa Chups, Coco Pops). Several brands named in the brief are listed under Gaps because I could not verify them.

## 1. Tone of voice and language: how brands speak to kids vs parents

### Takeaway
The brands that last speak in a **playful, conspiratorial, kid-slang voice on pack and online** ("tu", "kids", "trop fort", "journées qui déchirent"). In paid media, regulation pushes the same brands toward a **dual or family register**: adults allowed to be childish (Haribo Kids' Voices, Oreo "Stay Playful"), or parent-child bonding and learning (Kinder Applaydu). Humour, self-mockery and running jokes are the main tools in France.

### Cited Findings
**Malabar (FR, Carambar & Co, now Ferrero)**
- The official brand page speaks directly to children in familiar "tu" and slang: "Évidemment, tu connais Malabar !", "ses bulles légendaires… ses tattoos* inimitables", "la dose de bonne humeur assurée pour des journées qui déchirent !", "Avec Malabar, vivez le fun !". The brand line is "Le Bubble gum qui donne le sourire" / "Des bulles de sourire". — [Carambar & Co – Malabar](https://carambarco.com/marques/malabar/)
- The same page names playground rituals: "Qui ne s'est jamais amusé à coller des tattoos sur le bras de ses potes ? Qui n'a jamais surenchéri au concours de la plus grosse bulle ? Dans les cours de récré, c'est même devenu une tradition." — [Carambar & Co – Malabar](https://carambarco.com/marques/malabar/)
- The history timeline is titled "Raconte-moi son histoire !" and uses puns: "Mabulle, chat vaut le détour !", "Trop fort !", and the Minions "pour le meilleur comme pour le pire (surtout le pire !)". — [Carambar & Co – Malabar](https://carambarco.com/marques/malabar/)
- Historic slogan: "Quand y'en a marre, y'a Malabar !" — [Bonbon Factory](https://www.bonbonfactory.fr/quand-y-en-a-marre-ya-malabar/)

**Carambar (FR)**
- The voice is a riddle followed by a pun: "Qui est jaune, drôle et complètement barré ? À question évidente, réponse évidente : c'est Carambar !"; "Avoue, toi aussi, tu as déjà ri à ses blagues !". Even the stats are written as jokes: "82 000 km de Carambar… Pour une fois, on ne blague pas !"; "C'est le professionnel de la vanne !". — [Carambar & Co – Carambar](https://carambarco.com/marques/carambar/)
- 2017: the brand returned to TV with the line **"Carambar, c'est de la barre !"**, which "surfe sur l'humour et les codes des Youtubeurs". — [Carambar & Co – Carambar](https://carambarco.com/marques/carambar/)
- "Blague Carambar" has become a French expression for a bad or childish joke, and the jokes are still part of the brand's appeal. — [Babylangues](https://job-in-france.babylangues.com/french-language/blagues-carambar/)

**Haribo**
- Original German slogan "Haribo macht Kinder froh", dated to about 1930 by Haribo's official history. — [Haribo history](https://www.haribo.com/en-us/about-us/history)
- A French blog says the line began as "Haribo fait la joie des enfants", then gained "et aussi celle des adultes", and that the founder's son set it to the famous tune in 1950. Another source credits an unnamed employee with the 1930 slogan, so authorship is disputed. — [J'ai un pote dans la…](https://jai-un-pote-dans-la.com/anecdotes-de-marque-8-haribo/); [Dokumente/Documents 2011](https://www.dokumente-documents.info/fileadmin/uploads/tx_ewsdokumente/dd-2011-1-artikel-18.pdf)
- **Kids' Voices** (Quiet Storm, launched 2014 in the UK): adults in serious settings such as a boardroom, a library or a football sideline speak about Haribo in dubbed children's voices. There have been nearly 50 executions worldwide, including the US and Germany (the German spot was the 40th). — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/haribos-successful-uk-kids-voices-campaign-gets-us-launch); [Shots](https://shots.net/news/view/quiet-storm-creates-new-kid-s-voices-ads-for-haribo-germany); [System1](https://system1group.com/?p=17385)

**Oreo (Mondelez)**
- The "Twist, Lick, Dunk" ritual is the centre of **"Stay Playful"** (2019 onward). In the US, Wiz Khalifa and his son riffed on twist and dunk moves (Grammys 2019), framed as "no one is too old for fun". — [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2019/02/07/Oreo-s-new-Stay-Playful-campaign-taps-Wiz-Khalifa-and-his-son-to-show-no-one-is-too-old-for-fun)
- In the UK, Lewis Capaldi performed the ritual on camera to make it better known among 18–45-year-olds. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/creative-works/elvis-oreo-stay-playful)
- India's #AtHomeWithOreo (2020 lockdown) pushed parents to play with their kids. An Indian TV spot ("Disconnect to Connect") shows a girl sending her father an Oreo to pull him away from his laptop. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2020/06/mondelez-india-at-home-with-oreo); [MediaInfoLine](https://www.mediainfoline.com/advertising/oreo-urges-consumers-disconnect-to-connect-through-campaign)
- In the Philippines, the campaign is framed as family connection through play and quotes a pediatrician on the value of unstructured play. — [Malaya](https://malaya.com.ph/lifestyle/food/worlds-favorite-cookie-celebrates-108th-year)

**Kinder (Ferrero)**
- Kinder North America frames the brand as creating "special moments when parents can connect with their children". Applaydu was positioned as free "edutainment" that is ad-free and has parental controls. A "Go Wild Safari Series" on YouTube was made for parents, with 16 episodes featuring real families. — [Ferrero North America](https://ferreronorthamerica.com/news/kinder-joy-toys-come-to-life-with-applaydu)
- A print ad for Kinder Joy in India was noted for appearing to target parents rather than kids. — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/top-story/kinder-joy-appears-to-target-parents-not-kids-with-this-print-ad)

### Inferences
- The French players (Carambar, Malabar) keep a kid-to-kid "copain" voice: "tu", English loanwords ("kids", "fun", "tattoos"), puns, cheeky self-deprecation. The parent-facing message (heritage, nostalgia, "patrimoine français") sits beside it in the same text.
- Global players use **adult nostalgia or "inner child"** as the bridge (Haribo, Oreo), which lets them advertise in adult media and stay within the under-12 rules.

### Gaps
- I did not collect verified tone samples for Pez, Mentos, Smarties (Nestlé), Skittles ("Taste the Rainbow"), Hubba Bubba, Bubblicious, Chiclets, Lutti, Danonino/Petit Filous, Lunchables, Gerber, Tony the Tiger or Kinderini, because of the tool-call budget. Treat them as unresearched here.

## 2. Origin stories and founder myths, and how brands use them

### Takeaway
Founder myths are told as **short, funny "happy accident" or "humble kitchen" tales** and are reused on history pages, at anniversaries and on pack. The most useful precedents: Carambar's "erreur de génie", Haribo's acronym and kitchen, Chupa Chups' Dalí logo, and the Bazooka Joe comic.

### Cited Findings
- **Carambar**: created in 1954 by Georges Fauchille and Augustin Gallois, "d'après la légende, d'une simple erreur de recette. Chez nous, on appelle ça une erreur de génie !". Jokes have appeared on the wrapper since 1969, and the brand has published 5,000 of them. The site still asks the public to send jokes ("Je propose une blague"). In 2024 the 70th anniversary was marked with a contest for 70 surprise boxes. In 2025 a partnership with the Sapeurs-Pompiers de France ("Carahéros") put safety messages plus firefighter jokes inside the wrappers. — [Carambar & Co – Carambar](https://carambarco.com/marques/carambar/)
- Carambar sourced jokes through competitions in French schools, inviting pupils to submit them. — [Lingua Franca](https://www.mylinguafranca.com/le-chouchou-news/2021/9/11/blagues-carambar)
- **Carambar's "fin des blagues" hoax (March 2013)**: the brand announced it would replace jokes with educational games. A Facebook page and petitions appeared, and the factory town of Marcq-en-Barœul mobilised. On 25 March, earlier than planned and because of the scale of the reaction, the brand revealed it was a joke. Reported results were 55,000 tweets and close to 1,000 TV, radio and press mentions with no paid media (secondary source). The agencies were Kids Love Jetlag and Fred & Farid. — [ozap](https://www.ozap.com/actu/les-blagues-carambar-c-est-fini/446200); [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/lifestyle/magazine/2013/03/25/carambar-les-coulisses-dune-blague-5BVT2IS5FFB4FLUBELZUXSMICQ/); [Guichet du savoir](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/134780); [INA](https://www.ina.fr/ina-eclaire-actu/video/g816116001014/la-fin-des-blagues-chez-carambar-c-etait-une-blague)
- Carambar also ran a charity campaign, "La Blague à 1 franc", to turn old francs still in circulation into "smiles". — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-p3mAIOlAI)
- **Haribo**: founded in Bonn by Hans Riegel on 13 Dec 1920. The name stands for **HA**ns **RI**egel **BO**nn. Haribo's own history is titled "From a humble home kitchen to a global market leader". — [Vikidia](https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/Haribo); [Haribo history](https://www.haribo.com/en-au/about-us/history)
- **Chupa Chups**: Enric Bernat put a sweet on a stick because sweets were messy for children. In 1969 Salvador Dalí designed the logo, and the first slogan was "És rodó i dura molt" ("It's round and long-lasting"). Dalí reportedly suggested putting the logo on top of the lolly so it could always be seen intact. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chupa_Chups); [Print / Steven Heller](https://www.printmag.com/print-design-articles/dali-pops/); [Logo Design Love](https://logodesignlove.com/chupa-chups-logo)
- **Malabar**: launched in 1958 or 1959 by Krema. Collectible stickers or transfers were in the wrappers from 1959, TV advertising began in 1975, and the brand has belonged to Carambar & Co since 2017. — [fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_(chewing-gum))
- Ferrero (through Ferrara) took control of CPK / Carambar & Co (Carambar, Malabar, Krema, Lutti, Poulain…) on 31 Oct 2025, for about €240 million paid to Eurazeo. — [Wikipedia – Carambar & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carambar_%26_Co); [Paperjam](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/eurazeo-sells-carambar-and-co-to-ferrero-a-sweet-capital-gain)

### Inferences
- Origin myths work best when they are **self-mocking, short and repeatable** ("erreur de génie"). They are not used as solemn heritage.
- The Carambar 2013 hoax shows that a **ritual the audience owns** (reading the joke) is the brand's most valuable asset. Threatening to take it away proved how attached people were.
- Since Malabar and Carambar now sit under Ferrero, Kinder-style rules on targeting children will probably apply to these French heritage brands from 2026. This is an inference; I found no announced policy.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable primary source on the designer of Monsieur Malabar or the details of the 1969 character (fr.Wikipedia mentions a 1968 Jean-Claude Poirier link that I could not verify).
- Kinder Surprise's origin story (Michele Ferrero's purpose-driven framing) was not verified in this pass.

## 3. Mascots, mascot universes, rituals and participation mechanics

### Takeaway
The strongest kids' candy properties pair a **character** with a **physical ritual or collectible**: Malabar tattoos, Carambar jokes, Bazooka Joe comics, Oreo's twist, lick and dunk, Kinder toys plus the app. Mascots are swapped over time to stay current (Monsieur Malabar → Mabulle the cat → licensed Minions). Removing the ritual, as Bazooka did in 2012, tends to backfire.

### Cited Findings
- **Malabar mascot timeline**: in 1969 **Monsieur Malabar**, "un personnage blond, (très) musclé et vêtu d'un maillot jaune", became the face of the brand for close to half a century, and the tattoos arrived in the same period. In 2011 he "retired after 42 years" and was replaced by **Mabulle**, a black cat with sunglasses and a colourful tie whose mission is "officiellement, t'apporter un maximum de fun. Officieusement, faire le pitre !". In 2018 a Minions licence took over the packs for a year. — [Carambar & Co – Malabar](https://carambarco.com/marques/malabar/)
- fr.Wikipedia says the Mabulle switch (2011) was meant to appeal to a younger audience. — [fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_(chewing-gum))
- **Bazooka Joe**: in late 2012, after 59 years and with sales down from $17M (2007) to a projected $8.8M (2012), Topps dropped the comic strips for brainteasers and codes that unlocked videos and games on BazookaJoe.com. The VP said the aim was to make the brand "relevant again to today's kids". — [The New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/110868/bazooka-joe-comics-pulled-bubble-gum-after-58-years); [NPR via WQCS](https://www.wqcs.org/2012-11-30/say-it-aint-so-joe-bazooka-bubble-gum-is-killing-its-tiny-comics)
- The comics later came back because customers asked for them more than for anything else, and fans voted on a redesigned Joe, who then featured in comics, games and activities in packs. — [CBC Under the Influence](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6824358); [Comic Vine](https://comicvine.gamespot.com/articles/bazooka-candy-brands-reveals-new-bazooka-joe/1100-152452/)
- **M&M's spokescandies**: the 2022 redesign (green's sneakers, brown's lower heels) drew backlash. In Jan 2023 Mars announced an "indefinite pause" and brought in Maya Rudolph: "Now we get it — even a candy's shoes can be polarizing… the last thing M&M's wanted since we're all about bringing people together." The pause was a set-up for the Super Bowl, and the characters came back "for good" during the game in a spoof press conference. — [Food Manufacturing](https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/consumer-trends/news/22670912/mars-pauses-using-mms-spokescandies); [Fox47 / Scripps](https://www.fox47news.com/mms-spokescandies-return-super-bowl-ads)
- **Boomer (India, Mars Wrigley)**: **Boomer Man**, a blue-suited animated superhero who "rescues kids from sticky situations", led the ads for about 20 years with the jingle "Boom Boom Boomer". The brand built its own mascot rather than hiring celebrities. Around 2010 the target moved to young adults. In 2014 teenagers replaced kids in the TV spot, and Boomer Man was later dropped from ads and branding while the jingle stayed. Recent work: a DDB Mudra nostalgia relaunch ("Boom Macha De") and a Gen-Z campaign with cricketer Jasprit Bumrah ("Boom-rah"). Wrigley's Boomer also planned India's first bubble-blowing championship. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2020/09/brand-saga-boomer-advertising-journey); [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/we-want-to-make-chewing-gum-cool-inside-the-home-yogesh-tewari-mars-wrigley); [Agency Reporter](https://agencyreporter.com/mars-wrigley-boomer-campaign/); [Campaign Brief Asia](https://campaignbriefasia.com/?p=174886); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/wrigleys-boomer-plans-indias-first-ever-bubble-blowing-championship-32650.html)
- **Kinder Joy + Applaydu** (2020–21, with Gameloft and an education advisory board from Oxford's Department of Education): kids scan the leaflet inside the egg and the toy comes alive in the app through AR. The app is free, ad-free, has parental controls and adapts difficulty to age. The brand presents it as extending the surprise beyond the egg. — [Ferrero North America](https://ferreronorthamerica.com/news/kinder-joy-toys-come-to-life-with-applaydu); [Gameloft](https://gameloft.com/newsroom/gameloft-for-brands-and-kinder-launch-applaydu-in-4570); [Candy Industry](https://www.candyindustry.com/articles/89728-new-app-brings-kinder-joy-toys-to-life-through-ar)
- An MMA case study, "Kinder Gets Phygital", covers an app campaign in Iraq that used app features to identify parenting personas. The case ranked 7th of 118 entries; this is self-reported and paywalled. — [MMA](https://legacy-casestudies.mmaglobal.com/case_studies/view/91555)

### Inferences
- A recurring formula: **character + tactile ritual + collectible or sharing loop + a playground "competition"** (biggest bubble, best joke, tattoo swap).
- Mascot personality traits that worked: the cheeky troublemaker (Mabulle: "faire le pitre"), the kind superhero helper (Boomer Man), squabbling ensemble comedy (M&M's). Changes to how mascots look are risky, because fans treat characters as people (M&M's shoes).

### Gaps
- No verified data on how Mabulle performed versus Monsieur Malabar.
- I did not verify Tony the Tiger, Coco the Monkey's personality, Pez dispensers as collectibles, or Skittles' surreal "Taste the Rainbow" universe.

## 4. Regulatory and cultural shifts, 2018–2026

### Takeaway
Self-regulation (EU Pledge, IFBA) and national HFSS rules have pushed candy and cereal brands away from **direct-to-child media**. Mascots get repositioned or retired, campaigns use adult or family framing, and brands move into parent-approved formats such as edutainment apps and co-viewing YouTube series. Mascots can still be tied to HFSS products by regulators, as in the UK Coco Pops case.

### Cited Findings
- Ferrero has signed the EU Pledge since 2009. From 1 Jan 2017 it committed not to advertise to audiences that are more than 35% children under 12 (unless products meet EU Pledge nutrition criteria), across TV, radio, print, cinema and online including its own websites, and not to advertise in primary schools worldwide. — [FoodDrinkEurope](https://www.fooddrinkeurope.eu/?p=2237); [Access to Nutrition Index 2018](https://accesstonutrition.org/index/global-index-2018/scorecards/ferrero)
- Ferrero's 2024 policy documents use the IFBA threshold: "principally directed to children under 13" means media where more than 30% of the audience is under 13. Exceptions are allowed for products that meet common nutrition criteria. — [Ferrero 2024 policy PDF](https://www.ferrero.com/fr/sites/ferrero_fr/files/2024-04/4470.pdf)
- **Coco Pops (UK, 2018)**: the ASA first banned a Coco Pops Granola TV ad because the **monkey mascot** was seen as promoting the whole Coco Pops range, 60% of which was HFSS. After an independent review the ASA reversed the ruling, finding the granola (not HFSS) was the focus. Kellogg's had also reformulated the core range so it was no longer HFSS. — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2018/11/21/UK-watchdog-reverses-ban-on-Kellogg-s-Coco-Pops-advert); [RPC](https://rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/cocos-revenge-asa-reverses-kelloggs-hfss-decision); [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/kellogg-marketing-and-sales-company--uk--ltd-a18-1.html)
- M&M's 2022–23 changes (more inclusive redesign → backlash → "pause" → Maya Rudolph → return) show that mascot repositioning around values can become a culture-war flashpoint. — [Food Manufacturing](https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/consumer-trends/news/22670912/mars-pauses-using-mms-spokescandies)
- Boomer moved from kids to teens and young adults and dropped its kids' superhero mascot. — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/we-want-to-make-chewing-gum-cool-inside-the-home-yogesh-tewari-mars-wrigley)
- Oreo, Kinder and Haribo all frame recent work around **family play and connection** or an adult's inner child, not kid-only appeals. — [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2019/02/07/Oreo-s-new-Stay-Playful-campaign-taps-Wiz-Khalifa-and-his-son-to-show-no-one-is-too-old-for-fun); [Ferrero NA](https://ferreronorthamerica.com/news/kinder-joy-toys-come-to-life-with-applaydu); [System1](https://system1group.com/?p=17385)
- Carambar's 2025 firefighter partnership (safety messages in wrappers) is an example of purpose and prevention content added to a kids' ritual. — [Carambar & Co](https://carambarco.com/marques/carambar/)

### Inferences
- A pink monster mascot for a sugar-gum brand in France sits in Ferrero/EU-Pledge-style territory. Content that feels like family entertainment or co-viewing, with no direct "buy" calls aimed at under-12s, will be the safer norm.
- Values that are holding up: joy and smiles (Malabar "bulles de sourire", Haribo), play (Oreo), sharing, humour, and prevention or safety (Carambar Carahéros).

### Gaps
- I did not verify the status of the UK's 2025–26 HFSS TV/online ad restrictions, French rules (Loi Gattolin 2016 on kids' TV ads, the 2021 Climat/Studer laws on influencers and children) or Gulf/Maghreb rules. Another researcher should check these.
- I found no 2023–26 source on Kinder marketing in MENA beyond the Iraq MMA case.

## 5. Bubble-gum brands specifically

### Takeaway
Bubble-gum storytelling clusters around **bubble size and exaggeration** (bubbles lifting kids, parachutes, floating), **superhero or rescuer mascots**, **in-wrapper extras** (tattoos, comics, jokes), and **challenges or competitions**. Recent relaunches (Bubbaloo, Boomer) are TikTok-first, built on music and creators, and aimed at Gen Alpha and Gen Z.

### Cited Findings
- **Big Babol (Perfetti)**:
  - India, McCann: the long-running "Bade Kaam Ki Cheez" line. In a 2009 TV spot a boy uses a Big Babol bubble as a parachute to get back at a crow. A 2011 outdoor stunt showed a mannequin kid floating on a giant bubble. — [Campaign India](https://campaignindia.in/article/perfetti-rolls-out-campaign-for-big-babol/410314); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/out-of-home-news/big-babol-blows-one-big-bubble-43395.html); [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/big-babol-crow-india/mzju1yn6x38aj8cob4chq4tjuz)
  - Vietnam 2007, BatesAsia 141: a multicolour gum campaign aimed at kids aged 5–14, in which a chameleon tries to keep up with the coloured bubbles the kids blow. — [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/bubble-fun-campaign-marks-first-tv-export-from-batesasia-141-vietnam/202111)
  - Indonesia, DraftFCB Jakarta: a print campaign in which kids' bubbles overwhelm a toy car, a playground and a swing. — [PostKiwi](https://www.postkiwi.com/?p=3275)
  - Big Babol work has appeared in Cannes Lions archives. — [Love The Work](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/big-babol-223230)
- **Bubbaloo (Mondelez, LatAm)**:
  - Colombia: a TikTok relaunch after five years off the market, aimed at Gen Alpha and Gen Z. TikTok reports +24% brand recall and 1.4M interactions. — [TikTok case study](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/inspiration/Bubbaloo-halloween-posicionamiento)
  - Brazil, June 2023: "Tudo Pode Ser Mais Bubba" by DAVID, with 100% of the content co-created with TikTok, Instagram and Twitch creators. — [Nosso Meio](https://nossomeio.com.br/bubbaloo-lanca-campanha-com-foco-nas-geracoes-alpha-e-z/)
  - Mexico, Leo Burnett: "El Chiste es Bombear", a music-led, all-digital campaign with a "Bubbalow" challenge led by TikToker Dome Lipa. — [Insider LatAm](https://insiderlatam.com/?p=76906)
- **Boomer (India)**: superhero mascot, the "Boom Boom Boomer" jingle, a bubble-blowing championship, then a nostalgia and celebrity relaunch (see section 3). — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2020/09/brand-saga-boomer-advertising-journey)
- **Bazooka**: a comic in the wrapper (Bazooka Joe and Mort) from the early 1950s. It was removed in 2012, the brand declined, and fans later voted the comics back in (see section 3). — [The New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/110868/bazooka-joe-comics-pulled-bubble-gum-after-58-years); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6824358)
- **Malabar**: tattoos/transfers, "concours de la plus grosse bulle", Monsieur Malabar → Mabulle → Minions, "Quand y'en a marre, y'a Malabar". Internal figures show 162,000 and 50.8 next to unlabelled metrics. — [Carambar & Co – Malabar](https://carambarco.com/marques/malabar/); [Bonbon Factory](https://www.bonbonfactory.fr/quand-y-en-a-marre-ya-malabar/)

### Inferences
- The "bubble as superpower" trope (parachute, floating, overwhelming objects) shows up in markets and decades with no connection to each other. It is the genre's native visual metaphor and suits short vertical animation.
- Sound branding (the "Boom Boom Boomer" jingle, Haribo's tune) outlives the mascots, which is relevant for a brand called "Ballon Boum Boum".

### Gaps
- **Big Babol in France, the Middle East and the Maghreb**: I found no trade-press source on its campaigns, mascot or tone in these markets, despite its presence there. A follow-up in French or Arabic (e.g. Perfetti Van Melle Middle East social channels) is needed.
- Hubba Bubba ("Bubble gum: big bubble, no sticky"), Bubblicious and Chiclets were not verified in this pass.

## 6. Evidence on what makes kids' brand storytelling effective

### Takeaway
The evidence is mostly self-reported by brands and agencies. The patterns: (1) a repeatable format with a fixed device (Haribo Kids' Voices); (2) a ritual the audience owns, which creates earned media when threatened (Carambar 2013); (3) creator co-creation and music for Gen Alpha and Gen Z (Bubbaloo); and (4) removing the core in-pack story element hurts (Bazooka).

### Cited Findings
- Haribo Kids' Voices regularly ranked in the top 1% of ads tested with Millward Brown Link (agency and brand claim). Nielsen reportedly rated "Boardroom" the most impactful FMCG ad of 2015, and Haribo doubled media spend behind the platform. System1 scored the 2024 "Fishing" spot 4.0 stars, with exceptional short-term Spike and Brand Fluency but weaker long-term scores, and noted that viewers recognised Haribo despite light branding. — [System1](https://system1group.com/?p=17385); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/haribos-successful-uk-kids-voices-campaign-gets-us-launch)
- Carambar's 2013 hoax: 55k tweets and about 1,000 media mentions with no paid media (secondary source, no sales data). — [Guichet du savoir](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/134780)

### Inferences
- Effectiveness comes from **consistency of a distinctive device over years** (the Haribo voice swap, the Carambar joke, the Malabar tattoo, the Boomer jingle), more than from any single campaign.
- Failures cluster around **breaking the established relationship**: Bazooka removing comics, M&M's redesigning characters, Boomer dropping its mascot (outcome unclear). Self-aware recovery (the M&M's Super Bowl stunt, Carambar's "c'était une blague") can turn a misstep into earned media.
